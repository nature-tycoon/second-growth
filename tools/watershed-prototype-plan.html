Watershed prototype: implementation plan

Open the interactive lab

Stage one establishes a reusable, conservative water model and a controlled visual test. Normal gameplay, ecological calculations and saved farms continue using the existing systems. The prototype has no save-storage access.

Intended outcome

A player can see rainfall collect, compare infiltration against runoff, watch a creek move downhill, retain water behind a barrier, remove that barrier, and observe the stored water drain. An Amazon fixture demonstrates seasonal connection of an existing oxbow. A numerical budget explains where all water went.

State and units

StateRepresentationRole
Ground floorFloat64 elevation per cellFlat-floor topography; water head is floor plus surface storage.
Surface, soil, groundwaterFloat64 volume per cellPersistent water stores. Area is one, so surface volume equals depth.
Infiltration, capacity, baseflowFloat64 parameter per cellDifferent soil intake, saturation limits, and delayed local groundwater release.
Edges and barriersFour-neighbor topology; sparse barrier mapShared flow paths, fixed crest heights and optional permeability.
Sources and outletsExplicit cell/rate or crest/conductance recordsAccountable upstream supply and downstream exit.
Flow vectorsDerived arraysVisual arrows and ripple direction; recomputed on each outer step.
BudgetCumulative input/output countersInitial + rain + inflow − evaporation − plant use − outflow − current storage.

Time is measured in game days; elevations, volumes and rates use normalized prototype units. These are gameplay coefficients, not a calibrated flood forecast. The lab's terrain exactly displays the flat floors used by the solver.

Simulation sequence

  1. Validate finite, nonnegative forcing; subdivide time into steps of at most 0.05 days.
  2. Add rainfall and explicit sources, counting their external input.
  3. Infiltrate within available surface water and remaining soil capacity. Intake slows as soil fills.
  4. Move water above soil field capacity into the local groundwater store. Release groundwater by exponential reservoir decay.
  5. Apply evaporation to available surface water and plant demand to available soil water. Count actual removals, never requested removals.
  6. Compute every internal edge's desired flux from water head above the edge floor or barrier crest. Optional barrier leakage uses its permeability.
  7. Cap individual exchanges against equalization and donor storage; scale combined outgoing demands to prevent four-way drainage from overdrawing a cell.
  8. Apply all edge exchanges simultaneously. Only explicit outlets can remove water from the domain.
  9. Accumulate flow direction, update time, and expose the complete budget.

Depressions are never filled away by a drainage preprocessing step. A basin retains water until its head reaches an outlet. Boundaries are closed except at explicit outlet cells. There is no invisible maximum-depth clamp or unaccounted drain.

Fixtures and controls

The 48 × 36 creek valley has a supplied channel, compacted upland pasture, an initially empty disconnected basin, and a dam spanning the low valley. The Amazon fixture has a main river, an existing horseshoe lake and an elevated connecting channel. Identical resets use no random generator.

Controls adjust rain, upstream inflow, evaporation, infiltration and time speed. Pause, single-day stepping, reset, a twelve-day storm, dry weather, landmark inspection, rotation, zoom, soil moisture and flow overlays allow repeatable experiments. Storm duration is measured in simulation time and its end is handled at an exact step boundary.

Verification gates

  • Conservation across closed grids, rain, sources, outlets, evaporation, plant use and internal transfers.
  • Nonnegative finite stores, bounded soil capacity, still-water equilibrium, downhill flow and donor limits.
  • Basin retention below a sill, overflow above it, dam impoundment, overtopping, leakage and release.
  • Infiltration comparison, saturation, groundwater return and drying without over-removal.
  • Deterministic fixture replay, serialized state round trips and rejection of corrupt states.
  • Visual ground/head agreement, functioning controls, readable overlays and an error-free browser render.
  • A reported 120 × 90 timing check. This is a development measurement, not a promise for every browser/device.

Implementation files

js/sim/watershed.js is a pure engine with no Game, DOM, rendering or biome dependencies. js/render3d/watershed-view.js renders that state. tools/watershed-fixtures.mjs defines reproducible terrain. tools/watershed-lab.mjs binds controls to the engine; tools/watershed-check.mjs checks it in Node.

Integration after this prototype

  1. Agree on conversion between normalized water units and the existing World corner heightmap. Upgrade flat-cell storage to terrain-triangle storage curves before using smoothed game terrain.
  2. Add a World adapter, hydrology state migration, save versioning, and an explicit opt-in experiment. Preserve original land types separately from transient wetness.
  3. Advance water before moisture, habitat, plant stress and fish-passage calculations. Replace proximity-based moisture gradually using controlled map comparisons.
  4. Connect rendered water depth and flow to the existing terrain renderer. Update GPU buffers at simulation cadence, not every animation frame.
  5. Replace independent flood timers with persistent water exposure and species-specific stress. Define source/outlet forcing separately for each biome; reef tides require their own treatment.
  6. Connect beaver dam construction to shared-edge crests, leakage, repair and breach. Measure pond behavior and ecological succession before extending to other engineers.
  7. Add conservative sediment transport and bank evolution for natural oxbow formation. The present oxbow is pre-existing, and no new channel is excavated automatically.

Explicit simplifications

Groundwater is a local delayed reservoir, with no lateral aquifer flow. Plant use is fixed test demand. The dam is manually placed with a fixed crest. There is no momentum model, sediment, erosion, snowpack, animal engineering, vegetation response, fish passage or production-save integration in this stage. These limits keep the first model testable while leaving clear interfaces for subsequent work.
