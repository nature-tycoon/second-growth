Descriptions

About the game.

Short (one paragraph)

Second Growth is a free browser game about bringing damaged land back to life. Take a worn-out farm, a cleared rainforest or a bleached reef, plant natives, reopen creeks and migration routes, and see which of about 200 animal species come back. Then drop to eye level and walk among them.

Long

Every place in Second Growth starts out broken: an old dairy farm with a blocked salmon creek, a suburb paved over oak woodland, a reef cooked by heatwaves. Each of its seven landscapes is based on a real place, with its own plants, animals and damage to undo.

You decide where the effort goes: pull out invasive plants, reopen water, plant natives that suit the soil and light. Every plant and animal follows its own needs. Animals arrive from the surrounding land when there is food, water and cover for them. They graze, fish, stalk and chase, raise young, learn to avoid the places where hunters made kills, and leave again when the habitat stops supporting them. Beavers dam the creek on their own; the seasons, storms, fires and floods keep changing the ground.

Then put the tools down. Let the camera follow the wildlife, or drop to eye level and walk the ground you restored among elk, elephants and orangutans, through rainstorms, thunder and moonlit nights. The Ecosystem Report scores the land against the real ecosystem it is based on, and photo mode turns any view into a postcard.

Second Growth is made by one developer, Cole Weiler. It runs in any modern browser, on a computer or a phone held sideways, with nothing to install.

Features

What’s in it.

  • Seven real-world landscapes. Pacific Northwest, Amazon, Serengeti, suburban Atlanta, Nicaragua, the Great Barrier Reef and Sumatra, each with regional plants, wildlife and restoration work.
  • A living ecosystem. About 200 animal species and more than 200 plants, each with its own needs. Nothing is placed by a script: wildlife comes when the habitat is right.
  • From the map to the forest floor. Work the land from overhead, then drop in behind any animal and walk the ground at eye level.
  • Hunts, herds and a landscape of fear. Predators stalk from cover and chase; prey learn to avoid kill sites; carcasses feed scavengers and decay back to bones.
  • Water that matters. Pull a culvert and salmon return upstream; beavers build ponds that fill in to meadows over the years.
  • Seasons and disturbance. Rain, flowering and food come and go; storms, lightning, fires, floods and marine heatwaves can undo work.
  • Keystone moments. The elk rut, a lion’s charge, sea-turtle hatchlings running for the sea, monarchs on migration, happening on their own when the habitat supports them.
  • Watch mode. Put the tools down and let the camera follow the wildlife at a calm watching pace, with real field recordings for every landscape.
  • The Ecosystem Report. Six vital signs, a carbon estimate and badges, scored against the real ecosystem. Photo mode makes shareable postcards.
  • Free, anywhere. Plays in the browser on computers and phones, saves on the device, no account.
