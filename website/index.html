The game

Every place starts
out broken.

An old dairy farm with a blocked salmon creek. A suburb paved over oak woodland. A reef cooked by heatwaves. Each one has its own history and its own problems to fix.

You decide where the money goes: pull out invasive plants, reopen water, plant natives that suit the soil and light. Then watch what grows, check what each species needs, and change course when something doesn't take.

Animals come in from the surrounding land when there is food, water and cover for them. They feed, raise young, hunt each other, and leave again if the habitat stops supporting them.

An example from the Pacific Northwest

Reopening a salmon creek.

One save, two game years apart. Pull out one
culvert and the whole creek changes.

  1. 01

    Find what's in the way.

    A culvert under the farm road stops salmon from reaching the upper creek. With no shade on the banks, the water isn't good enough for them to spawn.

  2. 02

    Open it up and plant the banks.

    Tear out the culvert, clear the Himalayan blackberry, and plant native shrubs and trees along the water. Overlays show you fish passage, moisture and habitat as you work.

  3. 03

    Give it time.

    The plantings grow in and shade the creek. Open passage alone won't bring coho back. They need the clean, shaded water too.

How the ecosystem responds

You plant it.
Nature takes it from there.

Every plant and animal in the game follows its own needs. Your work changes the conditions, and the ecosystem sorts out the rest.

Vegetation

Plant communities develop over time.

Every plant needs the right moisture, light and soil. As trees grow and shade the ground, the plants beneath them change too.

Succession in the game

Pioneer plants help establish vegetation on disturbed ground. Later plant communities depend on what can grow in the changing conditions. A woodland, a savanna and a reef follow different regional rules.

Wildlife

Build it and they might come.

Animals settle where they find food, cover, water and a way in. Once there, they feed, breed, hunt and move on when the food runs out.

Arrivals and limits

Wildlife can arrive from surrounding habitat. Some species can be reintroduced through the game’s wildlife tools. Habitat capacity and population limits still apply; an introduction does not remove those needs.

Water & movement

Connections matter.

Good habitat behind a culvert or a fence is useless to the animals that can't reach it. Open the way and they can move in.

Inspecting the landscape

Use the game’s inspection tools and overlays to examine moisture, soil, shade, fish passage and species habitat. These views help explain why an intervention may work in one location and fail in another.

Season & disturbance

The seasons don't stop for you.

Rain, flowering and food come and go through the year. Fires, floods and marine heatwaves can undo work, and each landscape has its own.

Playing with uncertainty

Seasonal and disturbance rules depend on the map and difficulty. Observe what persists, what recovers, and where habitat needs further work as conditions change.

About the model

The game represents ecological processes through simplified rules and accelerated time. Its outcomes describe the simulation; they are not forecasts for real restoration sites.

About the project

An independent game,
still growing.

Second Growth is an independent game, updated several times a week with new species, behaviors and fixes.

If an animal acts wrong, a plant grows somewhere it shouldn't, or the game runs slowly on your machine, say so. The most useful reports name the map, what happened, and what you expected.

Playing Second Growth

Before you start.

Choose a landscape and a difficulty in the game. You can pause to inspect the habitat, plan your work, or watch individual animals.

What is Second Growth?

Second Growth is a free browser game about ecological restoration. Work with water, soil and native plants across seven landscapes, and observe how wildlife responds. It is an independent game in active development.

What do I need to play?

A modern browser with WebGL support. The game runs in your browser without installation or an account. A larger screen gives you more room to explore; touch controls are also available.

Will my progress be saved?

The game autosaves in your browser on this device. Return to the same website in the same browser to continue. Saves do not sync across devices. Private browsing and clearing site data can remove them.

How should I start?

Campaigns on supported maps introduce restoration work through guided chapters. Free Play makes every tool available from the start. The introduction and inspection tools explain the landscape and the actions available.

Can I adjust the pace and difficulty?

You can pause or change the simulation speed, and choose Relaxed, Standard or Challenging difficulty. Graphics and audio settings let you adjust the game to your device and preferences.

Where can I send feedback?

Use the project’s issue tracker to report a problem or suggest an improvement. Include the map and browser where relevant. A GitHub account is needed to post there.
