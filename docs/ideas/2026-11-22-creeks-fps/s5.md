# Session 5: creeks never bend sharply, fixed at the terrain cause

Decision record: [0054](../../decisions/0054-creek-and-shore-shape.md). Code: `js/maps/water-shape.js`.

## Done

- **Found the cause.** The ground is corner heights 8 m apart, and each tile is either water (corners
  carved down) or land, so the waterline ran along tile edges: pond shores were staircases, diagonal
  creeks zigzags, and a creek that turned on the grid turned 90°. PNW's ditch was laid as three straight
  legs with two square corners. The creek tool dug whatever tiles the mouse crossed, so a stroke's
  corner was a 90° bend.
- **Creek courses are curves.** PNW's ditch keeps its straight reaches but its corners are 32 m arcs
  (still passes the culvert). The creek tool steers: it follows the stroke a few tiles behind the
  cursor and cuts a sharp corner round, never tighter than a 32 m bend; started at a creek's end it
  carries on that creek's direction. Sumatra's canal grid keeps its right-angle junctions (real peat
  canals); junctions aren't counted as bends.
- **Shores follow smooth lines** (Cole's message: ponds too, from the terrain). A shore pass shapes the
  land at the water's edge so the waterline follows a smoothed outline of the water: it lowers bank
  corners into the inside of steps, lifts the outer corner of a step out of the water, steepens pond
  banks a little where the land behind still drains. It runs on every map as it's made and after every
  Landscape stroke (dig, fill, raise, lower). It never lowers a bed or changes a water level, never moves
  roads, buildings, dams, culverts or a graded pad. Exposed outer corners of water tiles are drawn as
  shore mud. Undo takes it all back.
- **Measured** on the drawn waterline (share of points turning over 60° within a tile), before → after:
  PNW 7% → 1.2%, Amazon 12% → 3.5%, Serengeti 13% → 3.6%, Atlanta 8% → 5.1%, Chinandega 9% → 8.0%,
  Sumatra 4% → 3.4%. Screenshots: the stock pond is now an oval, the ditch corners are curves, a dug
  creek winds with no right angles.
- **Shape rule in one place for session 6:** `sharpBends(w)` finds tight bends, `smoothCourse` +
  `rasterCourse` lay a new course, `shapeShoreNear(w, tiles)` reshapes the banks after.
- **New check** `tools/water-shape-check.mjs`: no sharp bend on any map as generated; waterline
  smoothness under a ceiling per map; scripted edits through the real tool path (right-angle and zigzag
  creek strokes, filling a creek tile, a pond dug in steps, Lower beside a creek); no water level moved.
- **Review fixes** (regression-reviewer): banks are shaped to a pond's full level, not its summer low;
  creek banks are never raised (that stopped beaver ponds spreading); only one water body's own outer
  corners are lifted; roads, culverts and Fill & grade pads hold; old PNW saves don't mistake the old
  ditch corner for player-dug creek; only the area near a stroke is reshaped.
- Checks: `water-shape-check` (new), `node tools/impact.mjs … --run` (all pass, motion sweep and intro
  film check included), beaver, Hollis, water, riverbank, fish, dug-creek, terrain-edit, terrain-relief.
  `calibrate all 15 3`: no fish or beaver line moved out of range; the other moves go both ways (seed
  noise on changed land). The perf guard's PNW fixture grows 71 animals instead of 60 on the new land and
  plants shift a few percent; baseline updated. The shore pass costs 12 ms per map at generation and
  0.2 ms per stroke.
- Fixtures moved: the beaver checks' dam site (54, 46) was on the old square corner, now (54, 48). A
  dam on a diagonal reach could report its empty pond's level above its crest; fixed in `level()`.

## Open questions for Cole

1. **Should old farms get smooth shores when loaded?** Now an old save keeps its jagged shores until
   you dig or reshape near them (heights are saved). Recommended: leave as is; reshaping a whole saved
   farm on load would shift the ground under plants and animals that already grew there.
2. **A creek stroke that turns back on itself, or ends in a turn tighter than a real creek, stops a few
   tiles short of the cursor.** Recommended: keep it (it's how the tool keeps bends realistic) and add
   a one-line hint the first time it happens ("Creeks bend gently: swing the stroke wider").
3. **Sumatra's drainage canals keep their straight lines and right-angle junctions.** They're real
   palm-oil canals, the thing the player blocks. Recommended: keep them straight.
4. **The dug creek lags the cursor by about 5 tiles while dragging** and catches up on release.
   Recommended: keep; if it feels odd in play, draw a faint preview line along the stroke.

## New ideas noticed

- Beaver ponds (Hollis model) grow and shrink their footprint by season, but the shore pass only runs
  at generation and after edits, so a beaver pond's new shore keeps the tile steps. A monthly shore pass
  over Hollis-managed water would fix it (cheap: only the basins that changed).
- What's still sharp: creek mouths and heads (a creek end is a square stub), bridges and culverts. A
  rounded creek head (the last tile tapered) would finish the look.
- Chinandega's crater lake and lagoon are barely improved (9% → 8%): its land doesn't drain (0044), and
  the lake's rim corners are often the tile's lowest. Worth a look when that map's terrain is next touched.
- The intro film (session 9) will see the new ditch curve and shores; its staging marks near the ditch
  corners should be checked when it's refilmed.
