# Session 8: frame rate — build session 7's list

Numbers and reasoning: [fps-plan.md](fps-plan.md), section 7.

## Done

- **Item 1, telemetry:** `perf_sample` now carries `work_p75_ms` (how long the slower frames'
  processor work takes) and `sample_s` (so shadow-map redraws read as a rate). Notes in
  `docs/posthog-player-perf.md`.
- **Item 2, GPU breakdown:** `PLAY_GPU_SPLIT=1` in `tools/play-profile.mjs`, run on Sharp and Fast on
  the four maps. Finding: on this Mac the browser's GPU timer reads 3–9 ms even with nothing drawn,
  so the "flat 9–12 ms of GPU" from session 7 was the timer, not real work. Timed properly (each draw
  waits for the chip), the chip's work is about 5–8 ms, and **only the number of pixels moves it**
  (half the pixels: about −3 ms). Shadows, cloud shadows and the ground are each within the noise.
  Also `tools/shadow-redraw-probe.mjs`: the shadow map redraws about 7 times a second by day and
  20 at night.
- **Item 3, fewer shadow redraws: not built.** A redraw costs nothing measurable on the M5, so there
  was nothing to win, and a coarser sun step risks shadows jumping at dusk.
- **Item 4, antialiasing off on 2x screens: built, measured, reverted.** Before/after on four maps
  showed no saving (median 0.0 ms; run-to-run noise ±2–3 ms). Apple chips smooth edges almost for
  free. No visual check was needed once it was reverted.
- **Item 6, the Fast notice:** a player who chose Sharp (or Balanced) and runs under 20 FPS for a
  minute gets one notice, ever, saying Fast would be much smoother. Their setting is never changed.
  Event `quality_hint`. 4 new checks in `auto-quality-check`.
- **Items 5 and 7: left open**, as planned (fps-plan.md section 7 says what each waits on).
- Checks: `perf-guard-check`, `auto-quality-check` (13), the 11 headless checks `impact.mjs` names,
  and `perf-analytics-browser-check` pass. `regression-reviewer` checked the plan and the diff. Its
  two findings are fixed: the notice is now a warning, so "important only" notifications can't
  swallow it; and the antialiasing question went away with the revert. Its diff review found one more: a Detail change now restarts the minute.

## Open questions for Cole

1. **Should the notice suggesting Fast come once ever, or once per visit?** Recommended: once ever
   (it's what's built). A player who chose Sharp knows their mind, and the point was "not nagging".
2. **Lower Sharp's sharpness slightly on 2x screens (2 → 1.75 pixels a point)?** Pixels are the
   only thing that measurably costs GPU time. This would cut about a quarter of them, at a barely
   visible softening on Retina laptops. Recommended: not yet. First see how the new step-up rules
   from session 7 do in PostHog over two weeks, then decide with those numbers.
3. **Can the older-hardware tester run one test?** `PLAY_NO_AA=1 PLAY_GPU_SPLIT=base node
   tools/play-profile.mjs pnw high 1`, then the same without `PLAY_NO_AA`, on an Intel HD/UHD laptop.
   Recommended: yes, if it's easy. Antialiasing and shadow redraws might matter on those chips even
   though they don't on a Mac, and that's the one thing this Mac can't measure.

## New ideas noticed

- The shadow map redraws up to every frame at night, because the night clock runs fast. If weak
  chips turn out to pay for redraws, a night-only coarser sun step is the cheap fix.
- `gpu_frame_ms` from Apple chips under Chrome is unreliable (the timer reads 3–9 ms with nothing
  drawn). The 16 ms step-up test in `autoQuality` uses it for capped players. On Apple it may say
  "busy" when the chip is idle and block a step up that would be fine. Worth checking in PostHog
  (`auto_quality` and `perf_sample` from Apple chips) before item 5.
- `play-profile`'s synced timing could replace the timer in the regular (non-split) output on Macs,
  so future before/afters here aren't read from a timer that doesn't measure the chip.
