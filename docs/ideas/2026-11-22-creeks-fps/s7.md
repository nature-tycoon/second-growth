# Session 7: frame rate — measure, design, first wins

Plan, numbers and session 8's build list: [fps-plan.md](fps-plan.md).

## Done

- **Goal restated by Cole mid-session:** "as many players as possible have a sharp 30 FPS
  experience" (35 was an arbitrary bar). Measure: share of players holding ~30 (median 27+) on
  Sharp. Today **29%** (45 of 157); 57% hold ~30 on some setting; 24% are under 20.
- **Measured** 30 days of players in PostHog by device, Detail, chip class and frame cap, plus every
  automatic Detail move: 55 players moved to Fast by chip name, 21 for running slowly, 13 stepped
  back up (6 undone). Just over half of players are held to 30 by the game (phones, laptops on
  battery), which is why a 35 median was never possible; with 30 as the goal that cap is fine.
- **Profiled** the four main maps at Sharp and Fast on the M5: processor drawing is small (2–5 ms),
  the batch's new overlay/lens/shore drawing doesn't show, GPU is a flat 9–12 ms in every view and
  Fast saves only 0–2 ms of it. Only top speed on Sumatra/Amazon falls under 60 on Sharp.
- **Design** from the `architect` agent (on Opus: Fable needs usage credits on this account).
- **Landed:** (1) the triangle and draw-call counters (they read 0 in many views because the
  lens and tree-card passes reset them); (2) players held to 30 can now step up a Detail level,
  judged by how long their frames take (both sides within 16 ms of the 33 a frame has); (3) on
  1x–1.5x screens Fast steps straight up to Sharp (Balanced is the same picture there); (4)
  `auto_quality` now records the work that decided it; (5) `tools/auto-quality-check.mjs`
  (9 checks). Notes added to `docs/posthog-player-perf.md`.
- Checks: `perf-guard-check`, `auto-quality-check` and the 10 headless checks `impact.mjs` names
  pass; play-profile Sharp before/after on four maps shows no slowdown and the counters reading
  in all 16 views (release-browser-check not run here; it runs on main).

## Open questions for Cole

1. **Fable for design work needs usage credits on your account** (it stopped with a usage-credit
   error). *Recommendation:* no need to buy them for this; the Opus design was solid. Enable them
   only if you want Fable for the bigger cross-system designs.
2. **Should phones count toward "Sharp"?** Phones (about a fifth of players) can't run the Sharp
   setting at 30 (4–9× the pixels). *Recommendation:* judge phones by "holding 30 on Fast" and keep
   them on Fast; count only computers toward the Sharp goal.
3. **Keep the 30 FPS cap on battery and phones?** It saves battery and heat and fits the goal.
   *Recommendation:* keep it.
4. **Antialiasing off on 2x (Retina) screens** (session 8 item 4): edges are barely smoother there
   and it costs GPU time. *Recommendation:* yes, after a side-by-side check.
5. **Shadows redrawn less often** (session 8 item 3): the shadow may move in small steps at
   dawn/dusk. *Recommendation:* yes if the visual check shows no visible jumping.
6. **Step down at 26 instead of 20** (session 8 item 5): more players helped sooner, a few
   might see Detail drop when they'd have been fine. *Recommendation:* yes, after two weeks of
   the new telemetry.

## New ideas noticed

- **Software-rendering players** (SwiftShader, llvmpipe, Microsoft Basic Render: the browser isn't
  using the graphics chip at all) run at 2–4 FPS. A one-time notice telling them how to turn on
  hardware acceleration in their browser could move some of the slowest tenth to 30 for free.
- **Apple Macs not held to 30 run slower (34 FPS) than their measured work suggests** (about 8 ms
  processor, 8 ms GPU). Probably Safari (no GPU timer, so the GPU number is from Chrome players
  only). Worth a look with a Safari profile.
- **Weak chips on battery** lose a third of their processor speed (22 vs 11 ms of work). A gentle
  "plugging in will make it smoother" hint after a slow spell on battery would cost nothing.
