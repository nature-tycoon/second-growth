# Session 6: big floods trim tight creek bends

Decision record: [0054](../../decisions/0054-creek-and-shore-shape.md) (Rules: "Big floods trim tight
bends"). Code: `js/sim/creek-cutoff.js` (new), `bendCutoff` in `js/maps/water-shape.js`, the hook in
`Events.finishFlood`.

## Done

- **What a flood does.** A big flood (strength 1.3 or more, about the top half of floods) may cut
  straight across the inside of one creek bend that's tighter than a creek bends (`sharpBends`): a
  chute cut-off, as real creeks lose a tight bend. The new channel is a rounded line a few tiles long
  (3 in the test, at most 8); the old loop stays on its old bed as marsh, an oxbow. Chance 0.5 per big
  flood, one bend a flood, so change shows over years.
- **Where it can happen.** Since session 5 the map's creeks and the creek tool never bend that tightly,
  so a fresh farm has nothing to cut. It reaches old saves (the PNW's old square-cornered ditch) and
  creeks a player's edits left sharp (say, a creek filled back to a corner).
- **What it never touches.** Land higher over the creek beside it than the flood rises (after the share
  your wetlands soak up, as the flood itself counts it); roads, trails, gravel,
  buildings, any feature (logs, boardwalks...), a level Fill & grade pad, locked ground; nothing within
  3 tiles of a beaver dam or its pond, a culvert, a boardwalk, a building, a road, a pond or a
  waterfall. A player's dug creek that already meets the bend limit is never cut.
- **Fish.** Before any cut the game tries it, checks with the fishes' own route test that they can
  swim the whole reach both ways, and puts it back if not. A bend turning up Hollis's slope is refused
  that way: the shorter cut would turn the corner's drop into a step. The new channel is graded like a
  dug creek, only its own tiles (a dug creek it joins keeps its bed).
- **Shown lightly.** The flood's notice adds: "It also cut a new channel across a tight bend of the
  creek, leaving the old loop as marsh." (Spanish too.) No cinematic.
- **Save/load** keeps the new course and heights; loading knows the channel as dug creek, as in play.
  Old saves load unchanged. Undo is cleared when a flood cuts (undo puts the whole height grid back,
  which would have pulled the new channel's ground from under it).
- **Random stream unchanged** where there's nothing to cut: every test runs before the run's random
  numbers are drawn, so seeded runs and calibrate play out as before.
- **New check** `tools/creek-cutoff-check.mjs` (8 checks): fresh maps have nothing to cut and draw no
  extra random numbers; a right-angle dug creek on the PNW is cut by big floods (bend gone, creek still
  runs head to ditch, fish swim it both ways, old loop marsh holding water, no other water level or
  height moved, ditch bed and culvert untouched, the notice); a bend fish couldn't pass is refused with
  the farm left byte-identical; the wetlands' share keeps water off; a small flood never cuts; a dam nearby stops it; a steered creek-tool
  creek is never cut; save and load.
- Reviews (regression-reviewer, plan and diff): trial cut before any random draw, grading only the new
  tiles, the wetlands' share counted, the oxbow cleared as the Shallow marsh tool clears a tile.
- Checks: `creek-cutoff-check` (8), `water-shape-check`, `terrain-edit-check`, `impact --run`,
  dug-creek, hollis-water, water, fish-water, beaver-pond, waterfall, species-name-count all pass;
  `calibrate pnw 15 3`: nothing moved since the last run.
- Changed from the brief: the cut is gated on height (land within the flood's rise over the creek),
  not on the flood's own flooded tiles, because the game's flood spreads only from creeks open to the
  river's fish: the PNW ditch above its culvert never floods, so no bend there would ever have been cut.

## Open questions for Cole

1. **Old PNW farms' square ditch corners.** They're the main thing this will round, but I couldn't
   rebuild an old save to test them; the upper corners sit near the stock pond and the lower one's
   ground may be too steep, so some may never be cut. Recommended: leave it; if you have an old PNW
   save, load it and let a few winters run (or I add a test from a real old save file).
2. **Should the flood's own map (what land floods) also spread along creeks above a culvert?** Now
   only fish-connected creeks flood their banks, which isn't how water works. Recommended: yes, as its
   own small change, with a calibrate run (it changes plant and tree losses along the upper ditch).
3. **Oxbow as marsh, not open water.** The old loop becomes marsh on its old bed. A real fresh oxbow is
   often a small lake that silts to marsh over decades. Recommended: keep marsh (simpler, sturdier; an
   open pond would need re-levelling and could leave mud).
4. **Chance and size.** One bend per big flood at 50%, at most 8 new tiles. Recommended: keep; if you'd
   like to see it more, 0.8.

## New ideas noticed

- Beaver ponds turning creek tiles to pond can leave a sharp pond-to-creek corner years in; a flood
  won't cut within 3 tiles of a pond, so those stay. A later pass could round them once a pond drains.
- A quiet journal line or a short-lived "new channel" marker on the map would let players find the cut
  (the notice points at the middle of the flooded ground, not the cut).
- The flood risk overlay could show where a big flood might cut a bend.
