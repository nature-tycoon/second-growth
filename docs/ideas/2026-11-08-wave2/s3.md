# Wave 2, session 3: Snakes

## Done

- **One snake body for all five snakes, each with its real build.** The snakes already shared one
  model and one crawl (`snake()` in `js/render3d/animals/aquatic-reptiles.js`, `slither` in the
  shader), but only the anaconda and python had been given their real length. Now every kind has a
  line in `SNAKE_BUILD`: body length, girth, head length, head width and height, and neck. As long
  as thick: anaconda 15 (unchanged, the shape Cole likes), python 23, boa 22, rat snake and garter
  snake about 30 (they had been 16 and 20, stubby). The python is a little longer (3.6 → 4.0 sizes).
- **Natural heads.** The head is laid out in real sizes, with more rings over it so it keeps its
  shape on a long snake. Boids (anaconda, boa, python) have a long, squared snout that widens to the
  jaw hinge over a slim neck (1.4-1.8 times the neck's width). Colubrids (garter, rat snake) have a
  rounder, more even head only a little wider than the neck, with bigger eyes. The jaw line runs from
  lip to hinge, eyes sit a third of the way back and set into the skull (on top for the anaconda,
  which watches from the water), nostrils and tongue scale with the head. The face markings now stay
  on the head instead of spilling onto the neck.
- **Anacondas and pythons really catch prey now.** Soaks showed snakes started hunts but never
  finished one: python 0 kills in about 40 hunts each, anaconda 0-1. They hunted like crocodiles,
  sliding toward prey from 12 tiles away at half a capybara's walking pace, and the prey saw them
  and walked off. Now every snake lies in wait: the anaconda and python at a bank where their prey
  drink, the land snakes near prey. They keep still and hidden and lunge only at prey within 1.6
  tiles. After a meal they lie up 5 days digesting. Rates (seeded): python 0.3 catches per snake-year
  (and pythons on the map went from 1.3 to 4.3, now that they eat), anaconda 2-6 catches in 8 years
  (roughly one per anaconda per year). The rule is in decision 0026; `tools/snake-hunt-probe.mjs`
  says why a snake does or doesn't catch prey.
- Checks: `snake-model-check` (now checks every snake's length-to-girth and that heads are wider

## Open questions for Cole

1. **Should anacondas be more common?** Usually there is one anaconda on the Amazon map, or none
   for the first years (the restored map averages 0.7-1.0). With so few, a catch is something you
   see about once a game-year if you're watching the right spot.
   *Recommendation:* raise the Amazon's anaconda numbers a little (more swamp habitat counted as
   anaconda habitat, or a slightly higher arrival rate) in a later balance session, not here.
2. **Show the coil-and-constrict on a catch?** It isn't in this session. Today a snake that catches
   prey eats it like any other hunter, lying beside the body. Coiling needs a new pose in the snake's
   shader (the spine wrapped round the prey), a medium job, not a cheap one.
   *Recommendation:* yes, as its own session: coil round the prey for a few seconds, then a slow
   swallow (the prey shrinking into a bulge that travels down the body).
3. **Garter snakes and rat snakes are now slimmer.** Real proportions make them thinner on screen
   at the usual zoom.
   *Recommendation:* keep them real; they read as snakes by their markings and crawl.

## New ideas noticed

- A swallowed meal as a visible bulge moving down a snake's body while it digests (the 5 days it
  lies up after a catch).
- Anacondas basking on banks in the morning and sliding into the water when something big comes by.
- Capybara (and other prey) now remember kill sites (0015), so after an anaconda's catch they should
  drink at another bank, and the anaconda moves its ambush. Worth a watch-mode check that this reads
