# Session 7: Serengeti soundscape, and the owed perf checks

## Done

- **The ever-calling bird calls at a natural rate.** It was the lilac-breasted roller. It is the Serengeti's only recorded bird, so every bird slot played it, about every 5–9 seconds. Each bird species now rests after a phrase (`BIRD_REST` in `js/audio/nature.js`): the roller waits 60–120 s, other birds 6–14 s. Check: `tools/nature-audit-check.mjs` ("A lone recorded bird calls now and then"), which gets 9–10 roller phrases in 10 minutes where there used to be about 130.
- **The day and night chorus plays, with real recordings** (downloaded after Cole's OK). Six habitat voices are `'*'` calls in `SOUNDSCAPES.serengeti`. They are common savanna birds the game doesn't model as animals, and they call more as the range recovers (`0.25 + 0.75 × health`):
  - ring-necked dove by day
  - emerald-spotted wood dove at midday (a new `midday` activity)
  - Hildebrandt's francolin at dawn and dusk
  - superb starling by day
  - red-billed hornbill in the morning
  - spotted thick-knee at night
  
  The village weaver now voices the existing `weaver` animal. A cicada bed (`bed-cicadas`, a new `cicadas` entry in `RECORDED_BEDS`) plays by day, loudest at midday. Crickets now play only at night on the Serengeti.
  - Credits are in `field-sources.json` (sha256 and clip windows), `recordings.js` (so Settings > Audio lists them), `LICENSES.md` and the README.
  - There is one clip per voice, about 0.7 MB in all. The sound-pack budget went from 6 to 6.5 MB (now 6.10 MB) in `field-audio-check` and `field-audio-browser-check`.
  - Clips were built with macOS `afconvert` plus a small Node trim, high-pass and level step, because there is no ffmpeg on this Mac. Levels match the existing bird clips, and the cicada level matches the cricket bed. Windows were picked by energy, and nobody has listened to them yet.
  - Checks: `field-audio-check`, `nature-audit-check` (new chorus check), `audio-startup-check` and `field-audio-browser-check` (decodes every clip in Chrome; the cicada loop passes the seam test) all pass.
- **Night layer.** Zebras bark at any hour now, not only by day. The insect bed carries on at night on the Serengeti (crickets), and is still loudest by day (the stand-in for cicadas).

Verdict:
- Simulation and processor drawing are unchanged and within budget.
- The GPU rose by about 1.5–2 ms in the Amazon's normal and close-up views in both rounds, which puts it at the 11 ms ceiling. That is likely the new fruit and flower shapes from session 6.
- The Serengeti GPU numbers move both ways between rounds, so they are noise.
- Not measured: the low-end Intel target.

## New ideas noticed

- Thick-knees, nightjars and frogs at waterholes could make a "night at the waterhole" layer, tied to `L.water`.
- Distant calls could get a slight delay with distance, as well as the existing low-pass filter, so a far lion roar sounds far.
- The cicada credit says "BioAcoustica (Martin H. Villet cicada collection)". The Commons page has no named author, and "MHV" in the filename is very likely Villet's collection. It is worth confirming on bio.acousti.ca.
- The bird rest per species could also come from the species data (call rate) rather than a list in the audio code, once more maps need it.
