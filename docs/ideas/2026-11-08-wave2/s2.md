# Session 2: reef start and anthias (Kalinda reef)

## Done

- **Four small healthy coral heads at the start** (`HEALTHY` in `js/maps/reef-world.js`): a "what you're restoring" preview on the reef flat, one of them just beside the starting view. Each is about 14 tiles: staghorn, table and plate coral round an old brain coral, with soft corals, barrel sponges and mushroom corals in the gaps, pink coralline algae underneath, and no turf. Two have a sea anemone (clownfish find them by habitat), two a giant clam. The colours come from the existing per-colony morphs, so they show the natural Indo-Pacific range (lilac and blue-green staghorn, gold and green plates). Living coral at the start goes from 199 to 259 tiles, against the 1,500 the coral score asks for, so the restoration goals still mean growing the reef back.
- **Scalefin anthias** (*Pseudanthias squamipinnis*), a new reef fish (`js/data/animals-reef.js`): orange-pink schools of 8 to 16, hovering a little higher than the chromis, feeding on plankton. Their habitat is living coral heads (less over staghorn thickets, which are the chromis's) and never bleached coral, so a heatwave thins them out. They arrive from the rest of the reef through habitat as the coral grows (`req` rises from 150 to 450 coral tiles), never bought. A school of 10 starts over the healthy head by the starting view. Coral trout, giant trevally and whitetip reef sharks hunt them. Drawn with the shared fish model (`kind: 'fish'`, lunate tail).
- **Schools drop into the coral when a hunter comes near** (`sprite.dive`, in `actors.js`): any hunter within 3 tiles, or a rush at the fish itself, and the school drops onto the coral of its own tile within about a third of a second, then drifts back up over a few seconds once the hunter has gone. Applied to anthias and chromis (the chromis's field-guide text already said it did this). Drawing only, so the simulation is unchanged. It costs one short loop over the hunters for each of these fish.
- Checks: `fish-water-check`, `reef-movement-check`, `reef-coral-clearance-check`, `animal-clustering-check`, `moments-check` and `animal-behavior-check` pass (its species count went from 202 to 203; `animal-behavior-audit.json` regenerated).
- Reef balance (idle reef game for 10 years, seeded with seeds 7 and 11, before and after): living coral at year 11 rose from 330 and 288 to 427 and 371 (the healthy heads seed the reef around them). Anthias settle at 26 to 48 and drop after a bleaching summer. Other fish stay within their normal range; chromis go up a little at the start, because the new heads have staghorn. Start score 1.6 → 8.8 and year-11 score 48 → 49 to 50. Simulation cost per day: 2.8 → 2.9 ms.
- Profile (`play-profile reef fast 2`, HEAD vs this change, on battery with load around 3): 60 fps in every view before and after. The grown reef now holds about 50% more animals (334 vs 211 in the normal view, because there is more coral and the anthias), and animal drawing stays about the same per frame: play 0.11 → 0.18 ms, wide 0.54 → 0.51, close 0.24 → 0.23. Simulation per frame rose with the extra animals (play 0.5 → 0.8 ms, top speed 2 → 2.5).
- Checked in the browser: a new reef game shows the healthy head beside the starting view, with the orange anthias school over it. Marking the school as fleeing took every fish to a full dive.
