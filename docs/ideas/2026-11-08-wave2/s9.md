# Session 9: Terrain slope proposal (design only)

## Done

- **[docs/proposals/terrain-slope.md](../../proposals/terrain-slope.md)**: how height works now, relief measured on every map, what each real landscape looks like, three options (scale the relief, a landform recipe per map, a generated drainage network) and the checks a build session would run. No game code changed.
- Main findings: every map but the reef and Chinandega uses the same PNW valley tilt underneath, so the Amazon, Atlanta, Serengeti and Sumatra all have about 16 m of relief across a ~1 km map (median grade 2.5–4%); the 3D view is true to scale (a tile 8 m, a level 2.4 m). Water already mostly runs downhill (about 3 pits per 1,000 land tiles). Saves store heights, so old games keep their land and nothing needs migrating.

## Open questions for Cole

1. **Which option?** *Recommended: B, a landform recipe per map with a "drains downhill" pass, built one map at a time starting with PNW. It looks like each real place, costs no frame time, and is the groundwork water flow needs later.*
2. **How steep should PNW get?** *Recommended: 40–80 m from river to the north edge, with the farm on a flatter valley floor and the creek in a small ravine. Real foothills are steeper, but steep slopes everywhere would make placing plants and watching animals harder.*
3. **Should the Serengeti change at all?** *Recommended: only shallow drainage lines (korongos) and perhaps taller kopjes; keep its grades under about 4%.*
4. **Should old saves get the new land?** *Recommended: no. Only new games get it; old saves keep the land they were made on.*

## New ideas noticed

- The Amazon would read better as two levels, a flooding várzea and terra firme behind a bluff, than as the PNW tilt it has now. That also makes the high-water season more visible.
- Seasonal Serengeti drainage lines could hold water for a few weeks after the rains, giving wallows and waterholes a natural place.
- The relief measuring script could become `tools/terrain-relief-check.mjs`, a per-map guard that keeps grades in band and creeks running downhill.
