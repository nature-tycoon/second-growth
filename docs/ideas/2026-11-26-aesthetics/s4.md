# Session 4: light instead of overlay washes, water at golden hour

Items 6 and 7 of the [aesthetics plan](../../aesthetics-plan-2026-11-26.md). One shared uniform in the
existing shader patch and constants: no new shader programs, no geometry. Rule recorded in decision 0058.

Pictures: `s4/<map>-<hour>-<before|after>.jpg` (before = s3's commit a07d62a), release forest scene,
High, zoom 0.62, **with the 2D effects layer drawn** (`node tools/value-capture.mjs <dir> <maps>
--hours=golden,sunset,night --fx`; seasons with `--month=7` PNW autumn, `--month=4` Serengeti dry
season; the creek with `--water`). Brightness percentiles: `s4/values.json`.

## Done

- **The washes are light now.** The season tint, Serengeti's dry-season glare, the golden-hour wash and
  the night's navy veil were translucent fills over the whole picture on the 2D layer. They're now one
  multiplier on every lit material's diffuse light (`haze.uLightTint` in `withClouds`,
  `js/render3d/atmosphere.js`), worked out each frame from the same colours (`washInto`, `WASH_*` in
  `js/render3d/scene.js`). Specular is left alone, so the water keeps its sparkle at night. Off at eye
  level (it never had the washes). The reef's sea surface, carcass bones and the perch bough now take
  it too.
- **What changed in the picture:** medians match the old pictures within 1–3 (of 255) at golden hour,
  sunset, night and in both seasons; the highlights are equal or a touch brighter (Serengeti dry season
  95th percentile 171 → 172), and the darkest shadows are deeper (PNW autumn darkest 5%: 34 → 30),
  because a fill lifted the shadows and greyed everything, and light doesn't. Night reads clearer and
  less hazy (`pnw-night-*`). Tuned to match: the night at 0.12 strength and the season tints at 0.6
  (`WASH_NIGHT`, `WASH_SEASON`): the first tries were much darker at night (median 26 instead of 46)
  and yellower in the dry season.
- **The backdrop still matches the far edge**: the melt and haze colours get the tint once, before
  `screenColor` (0058).
- **Night vignette**: the veil was darker toward the edges; the existing vignette goes from 0.8 to full
  at night instead (no extra drawing).
- **Water at golden hour**: a low sun ahead already turns a creek to a white sheet, so its highlight gets
  sharper rather than brighter toward golden hour, sunset and dawn (shininess up to ×2.5, specular
  ×1.15: `WATER_GLINT`), breaking the sheet into glints (`pnw-creek-golden-facing-sun-*`). In the usual
  view the low sun is mostly behind the camera, where no reflection can reach it, so the little 2D
  sparkles on open water also come out more often and in the sun's gold at that hour
  (`SPARKLE_GOLD`, `pnw-creek-golden-*`).
- **Cost:** three full-screen fills fewer on the 2D layer (the season tint was drawn every frame, even
  at strength 0). The 2D layer's CPU time was 0.1 ms before and is the same after, so the saving, if
  any, is GPU fill on weak laptops, which headless Chrome can't measure. The shader adds two multiplies.
- `tools/value-capture.mjs`: `--hours`, `--month`, `--fx` (draw the 2D layer: `release-scenes` leaves it
  out, so s2's and s3's pictures never showed the washes) and `--water`.

Checks: `horizon-browser-check` (midday, golden hour, night; now also asserts no light tint at eye
level, even from night), `watch-check` (16), `perf-guard-check`, `intro-film-check --live` (mean
difference 15.8 of 255 from the film's last frame), `check-release --headless` (154 scripts) pass;
`first-frame-profile` 28 programs on PNW, the same as the base commit. `release-browser-check` passes
but reports 15 scenes changed (PNW forest 45% of pixels, mean 21): its references predate s2, so this
is the batch's look so far (its scenes don't draw the 2D layer, so s4's share there is only the
season tint). Refresh them once, after looking, at the batch's end (`--update-visuals`).

## Open questions for Cole

1. **Night brightness.** Night now matches the old median but with deeper shadows and no navy haze.
   *Recommendation:* keep. For a bluer night raise `WASH_NIGHT`'s strength (0.12) a little; for a
   darker one, the same (it darkens and blues together).
2. **Season tint strength** (`WASH_SEASON` 0.6). At full strength Serengeti's dry season and PNW's
   autumn went clearly yellower than before. *Recommendation:* keep 0.6; raise toward 1 if you want
   the seasons to read more strongly.
3. **The diagonal "sun wash" and the vignette stay on the 2D layer** (`ambience.drawSky`): the sun
   wash is a screen-position gradient (strongest top-left), which light can't copy, and both are drawn
   at eye level too. *Recommendation:* leave them; moving the sun wash would change eye level, which
   this session kept as it was.
4. **Rain and cloud dimming fills** (4% grey over the screen) are still 2D washes. *Recommendation:*
   move them the same way in a later pass; small and weather-only, so not worth the risk here.
5. **Reef still pale green at play zoom** (from s3). Not touched: the sea's own colour under the light,
   not a wash. *Recommendation:* a deeper `look.underwater.shallow` in s5 or later, looked at alone.

## New ideas noticed

- The usual view almost never shows the sun's reflection on water, because the sun sits behind the
  default camera for most of the day. A sun that swings round to face the camera at golden hour
  (`tod.sweep`) would let creeks flash for real; it changes every shadow, so it's a taste call.
- The release scenes stub out the 2D layer, so release baselines never show fireflies, sparkles or the
  vignette; a `--fx` variant of a few release pictures would catch 2D regressions.
