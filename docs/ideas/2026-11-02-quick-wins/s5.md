# Session 5: reef life (Kalinda reef)

## Done

- **Fish keep clear of coral by body size, one rule for every reef fish** (`coralFloor` in `js/render3d/flight-clearance.js`, used in `actors.js`). In the simulation, coral was never an obstacle: fish crossed any sea tile and were drawn at their own depth, often inside a staghorn thicket. Now:
  - A fish small enough to shelter (size 6 or less: clownfish, chromis, cleaner wrasse, hawkfish) rises only over coral on its own tile. It can sit down in an anemone or a gap next to a thicket, but never inside the branches.
  - A bigger fish (and turtles, sharks, rays) checks a crown's width around it and a little ahead along its heading, and keeps its body depth over the top.
  - Sea-floor animals (sea cucumbers, urchins) are left alone. Fish are never drawn above the surface.
  - Only the drawing changed, so the simulation and balance stay the same. The lookup runs every 4th frame per fish, and the lift eases between lookups.
  - New check `tools/reef-coral-clearance-check.mjs` grows a dense coral garden and runs the fish for 2,400 steps (120 game days). Result: 3,600 samples, none with too little room under the surface, no fish stuck.
- **A faint puff when a fish eats a fish.** `game.onPredation` records fish-on-fish catches in a fixed ring of 8. The overlay draws up to 6 soft, dark red-brown blobs from a fixed pool of 30. They drift apart and fade in 1.2 to 2 s. No memory is allocated per catch. Checked in the browser: a coral trout catching a chromis shows a small, faint cloud.
- **A more colourful reef, in natural tones.** Each coral, soft coral, sea fan, sponge, anemone and clam species now grows in 3 to 5 real colour morphs (`look.morphs` in `js/data/plants-reef.js`). Each colony keeps one morph, picked from its tile (`morph` in `flora.js`). Examples: staghorn in tan, brown, lilac, blue-green and yellow-green; sea fans in red, orange and gold; barrel sponges in rust and purple. Bleaching still turns them white. Only the instance colours changed, so the cost is zero.
- **Clean horizon at eye level.** At eye level above the water, the open sea faded into the pale sky haze across a wide band, so the horizon looked grey. On a clear day the sea now fades to a deep tropical blue, which leaves a crisp pale line at the horizon under the clear `SKY_BLUE` sky (`uSeaFar` in `sea.js`). In cloud and rain it goes back to the haze. Seen from under the water, nothing changed.
- Checks: `fish-water-check` and `reef-movement-check` pass, along with the new coral clearance check. `fish-swim-check` passes too.
- Profile (`play-profile reef fast 2`, HEAD vs this change; animal drawing ms): play 0.10 → 0.11, wide 0.59 → 0.61, close 0.16 → 0.13. Everything runs at 60 fps. Plant drawing is unchanged within run-to-run noise (top speed swings 0.35 to 0.73 ms between runs of the same code). Before the lookup was limited to every 4th frame, wide view cost 0.98 ms.
