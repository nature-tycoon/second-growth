# Session 4: movement glitches, shared rules

## Done

- **Legs follow the ground, for every walker.** The leg clock ticked at walking speed even when an animal barely moved, so an elk braking behind its herd or edging round a trunk strode on the spot. Now the drawing measures how far each walker really moved and slows and shortens its stride to match. Under about a third of its pace it stands with a slight shuffle (`js/render3d/stride.js`, used in `actors.js`). Only the drawing changed, so the simulation and headless results stay the same.
  - Walkers drawn striding at under a fifth of their pace: 11% of walking frames before, about 1% after (pnw and serengeti, through the real `Actors`).
  - Cost: a few arithmetic operations per visible walker per frame, no allocation.
- **Why elk crawl.** `tools/gait-pace-probe.mjs` shows elk held up on about half their walking steps, most of any species. They are mostly braking behind a herd mate in single file (`spacing.steer`), which is real. The fix there is the stride rule above. One small cause fixed in the simulation: a herd follower that stalls near the end of its walk (a mate or trunk on its spot) now stops there, instead of pushing on the spot for up to 3 s before giving up.
- **Smoother flying over trees, for every flier.** A bird now looks about a second of its own flight ahead (1.4 to 4 tiles, sampled every 0.7 tiles so no crown is skipped). It eases up over trees and sinks back more slowly, so a forest is one smooth swell, not a hop per crown. Landings, stoops and dives keep their quick descent. The sudden changes in climb rate (`jolt` in `tools/flight-canopy-check.mjs`, new) dropped about 2 to 4 times on all four forested maps. Birds inside crowns stay under the 1% limit (0.5 to 0.73%, before 0.07 to 0.67%).
- **Salmon leap check:** `tools/animal-locate-check.mjs` now expects the new 0.3 tile-height leap (camera range over 0.2, was 0.5). The code was right; the check was stale. All 5 locate checks pass.
- Checks: `animal-locate-check` passes. `flight-canopy-check` passes, with the new jolt limit (HEAD fails it on 3 of 4 maps). `animal-steering-check` passes on every map except Atlanta with the default seed 1987. That failure (7.02 antler racks in trunks per 1000) is identical on HEAD, so it came before this session. With seed 91, Atlanta and pnw both pass.
- Balance: unseeded 5-year `balance-sim restore` before and after shows only run-to-run noise (one run had a fire); the follower rule moves herd positions, not populations.
- Decisions updated: 0025 (stride follows the ground) and 0030 (flight clearance easing).

### Playtest fixes (Cole's feedback on the branch)

Measured headless with `tools/follow-height-probe.mjs pnw robin,woodpecker,coho`: 6 of each on a restored map, drawn by the real `Actors` at 20 frames a second and normal game speed. Tile-heights per frame, before → after:

| | worst height change | 99th percentile | jolt 99th percentile (what looks jerky) | frames with jolt over 0.1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| robin | 1.61 → 0.59 | 0.66 → 0.35 | 0.59 → 0.09 | 4.9% → 0.9% |
| woodpecker | 1.36 → 0.59 | 0.47 → 0.19 | 0.50 → 0.11 | 3.6% → 1.1% |

- **Jerky followed birds, root cause:** the height a bird aims for jumps 2 to 4 tiles when it takes off from a perch (to cruising height over the canopy), when it starts its landing, and when it grabs a trunk. The drawing closed 42% of that gap every frame, so a robin popped up to 1.6 tiles in one frame. Now every bird's drawn height has a climb rate that builds and fades, capped at a slant to its speed (`easeBirdHeight` in `flight-clearance.js`). Take-off, climbs and landings are swoops.
- **Woodpecker near-teleport:** two causes. The height pop above, and the bird's position snapping between the bark and its flight position when it lets go of the trunk (7 sideways jumps over a quarter tile in one frame, now 0). Any bird leaving or reaching a trunk or a snag top now glides that gap over about half a second.
- **Salmon bouncing in place:** not the 0.3 height. Leaps ran on a fixed clock every 2.4 to 4.5 s, whether the fish was swimming or holding still. Now a salmon leaps only while it's actually moving along the river. Holding still, it stays under. Leaping frames with the fish not moving: 2286 of 4103 → 363 of 1975. Those 363 are slow swimmers under the probe's threshold, or a leap finishing as the fish slows. `animal-locate-check` now tests both cases, and HEAD's code fails the "holding still" one.
- `flight-canopy-check` gives a bird 0.6 s after take-off to fly out through its own crown. Real birds leave a perch through the leaves, and the old drawing only passed by popping straight up. After that window, birds inside crowns: 0.08 to 0.33%, below before (0.5 to 0.73%).
- Checks: `animal-locate-check` (5 pass), `flight-canopy-check` (pass), `animal-steering-check` pnw and serengeti (pass).

### River fish stuck in one stretch (Cole's second playtest)

- **Not caused by this batch.** `tools/river-fish-roam-probe.mjs` (new) tracks each river fish for 8 game days (about 11 minutes of play). It counts a fish as stuck in a 2-day window if all its positions fit in a box 1.5 tiles across. Main and this branch gave identical positions: 57% stuck for cutthroat, 60% for coho. Session 5's coral rule only lifts reef fish over coral (sea only), and my leap change is drawing only. My leap change did make it easier to see, though: idle salmon used to bounce in place, and now they hang still.
- **Root cause:** river fish waited like land animals. After arriving anywhere they idled 0.5 to 3.5 game days, and when their own tile looked best they waited 1 to 4 more. That's minutes of play hanging in one spot, while a deer at least grazes. Now every river fish holds 0.1 to 0.4 days, then noses on to a nearby spot. Stuck windows on pnw: cutthroat 57% → 27%, coho 60% → 17%, about twice the distance covered. Atlanta's bluegill and bass don't change much (around 55 to 70% either way), because they live in small ponds.
- Not touched: the reef (all reef checks pass), water and beaver files. Checks: `fish-water-check`, `fish-swim-check` (4), `fishing-check` (8), `reef-movement-check` (6), `reef-coral-clearance-check` all pass.

### Fish grouped together for years (Cole's third report)

- **Root cause, older than this batch:** creeks running downhill are drawn as a staircase of pools, deep at the bottom of each tile and only a few centimetres deep at the lip into the next. A river fish refused any step into water under 7 cm, and a separate rule put any fish in shallow water back into the deep pool. A fish swimming up or down the creek pressed against one lip forever, and every fish heading the same way piled up behind it. On pnw, all 10 coho were on 3 tiles after 2 game years.
- **Fix, for every river fish:** blocked by shallows, a fish first tries headings to either side that stay in swimmable water. If it's still held up after half a day, it hops the lip to the first swimmable water within a third of a tile, like a trout or salmon leaping a riffle. It never rests in the shallows.
- **Result:** after 2 game years the coho use 10 tiles, spread about 20 tiles across, and stay like that through 4 years. Cutthroat use 10 tiles instead of 5. Atlanta bass stay spread through 3 years. `fish-water-check` has 0 fish in too-shallow water on every map (before, 6 per 17 520 on one pnw seed). New tool: `tools/river-fish-spread-probe.mjs [map] [years]`.
- A first attempt let fish linger in the shallows while crossing. That failed `fish-water-check` badly (up to 17% of samples in Chinandega, some fish on land), so I replaced it with the hop.
- Checks: `fish-water-check`, `fish-swim-check`, `fishing-check`, `reef-movement-check`, `reef-coral-clearance-check`, `animal-locate-check` and `animal-steering-check` pnw all pass.

## New ideas noticed

- Trout and salmon could hold station facing upstream in the current between moves (drawn facing the flow, sculling), which would read as natural rather than parked. Needs the river's flow direction per tile.

- `gait-pace-probe` shows other species held up as often as elk: impala, zebra, giraffe and elephant around 55 to 70% of walking steps in the Serengeti. The stride rule hides this visually, but herds there may be moving slower than intended.
- Amazon kingfishers make up all of the Amazon's in-crown samples (fishing from perches inside trees). A perch-exit rule for fishers would tidy that.
