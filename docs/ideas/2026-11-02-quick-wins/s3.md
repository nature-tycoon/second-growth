# Session 3: follow-cam hiding, one shared rule

## Done

- One rule in the tracking lens (`js/render3d/focus.js`) for every species and map. Near the animal, it hides only what lies between the camera and the animal's own body box. It never hides what the animal stands, perches, climbs or swims on.
  - The trunk an animal clings to stays (woodpeckers today), unless the animal is round the back of it.
  - Anything below the animal's feet that shows below it on screen stays: palm fronds under a monkey, coral under a fish.
  - The wide "clearing" now opens gradually toward the camera, so the plant the animal is in stays. Before, it opened at full width right at the animal.
- Leaves in front of a monkey still open: all 80 views in `canopy-reveal-browser-check` still pass (the langur stays at least 98% visible).
- New check `tools/follow-support-browser-check.mjs`: woodpecker on a fir, langur on an oil palm and blue tang over Kalinda coral, each from four sides. Screenshots are saved in `tools/release-output/follow-support-*.png`. The old code fails it: half the woodpecker's trunk was hidden.
- Cost per frame: one 4×4 inverse per frame on the CPU, plus a box test of about 20 operations per pixel, only while an animal is tracked.

## Open questions for Cole

1. When a woodpecker is round the far side of its trunk, the lens still opens a hole in the trunk so you can see the bird. Keep that, or leave the trunk solid and let the bird be hidden? **Recommended: keep the hole.** Following a bird you can't see is worse.
2. The clearing around a tracked animal is now tighter near it, so more of the tree it's in shows. Is the forest framing still open enough when you follow a deer through thick brush? **Recommended: keep it.** If it feels cramped, it's a one-number change (`2.0 * uFocusR` in focus.js).

## New ideas noticed

- `tools/animal-locate-check.mjs` fails before and after this change, on the salmon leap height (spread over 0.5). This is probably session 1's lower salmon leaps. The check's threshold should be lowered to match.
- Monkeys that climb up trunks (not woodpeckers) could set the same `st.trunk` as their support. Right now they rely on the body-box rule alone.
