# Session 4: frame-rate follow-ups

## Done

- **Software-drawing notice** (`js/ui/perf-notices.js`, wired into the frame loop in `js/main.js`).
  When the browser draws without the graphics chip (SwiftShader, llvmpipe, Microsoft Basic Render
  Driver), 4 s after the farm shows the game says once, ever: "Your browser is drawing the game
  without your graphics chip, so it will run very slowly." followed by the steps for Chrome, Edge or
  Firefox (a general line for other browsers) and "If it's already on, updating your graphics driver
  can help." It stays up 30 s and is in the journal. Event `software_render_notice` (`gpu`,
  `browser`). PostHog saw 2 such players in 60 days.
- **Battery hint**: on Fast, on battery, clock running, under 20 FPS for two minutes in a row, once
  ever: "The game is running at about N frames a second on battery. Plugging in will make it
  smoother: computers slow themselves down on battery." Event `battery_hint`. Changed from the brief:
  it counts the measured slowness on any chip rather than only chips named weak, and only on Fast,
  so a player on Sharp never gets this and the "try Fast" notice back to back.
- **Apple GPU timer: it could block step-ups, now ignored on Apple chips.** Under Chrome it reads
  10–16 ms (one sample in seven over 16) where the chip's real work is 5–8, so the minute of passing
  3-second spells the 16 ms test needs rarely happens. Few players were affected (2 Apple + Chrome
  players were moved to Fast in 45 days). On Apple chips the step-up for a frame rate held to 30 is
  now judged by the processor's work alone, Safari included. The two-minute trial still undoes a
  step that's too much. `auto_quality` carries `gpu_ignored: true` then. `auto-quality-check` 13 → 17.
- **play-profile on Macs** prints synced timing (`synced N ms (GPU timer M, unreliable here)`) for
  every view, with `syncedMs` in the JSON; `PLAY_SYNC=1/0` forces it on or off.
- Both notices checked headless: `tools/perf-notices-check.mjs` (9 checks: right names, each
  browser's steps, once, not while hidden, not plugged in, unknown, paused or on Sharp, a broken or
  short spell starts over).
- Not checked by eye in the browser (Cole's machine has a real chip and is plugged in, so neither
  notice can appear naturally); the wording and conditions are covered headless.

## Open questions for Cole

   Before, Safari (no GPU timer) never could. Recommended: keep it; the two-minute trial undoes a
   step that's too much, and Safari Macs mostly run uncapped anyway.
2. **Should the battery hint also show on Sharp/Balanced for a player who turned down the "try
   Fast" notice?** Recommended: no; one notice about speed is enough, and Fast is the bigger help.
3. **Check in two weeks (about 2026-12-07)**: how many `software_render_notice` players come back
   with real-chip `perf_sample`s, and how often `battery_hint` fires. Recommended: look at it with
   session 7's step-down review the same day.

## New ideas noticed

- Players whose browser draws in software (5 FPS) still get the full intro film and the heavy start;
  the game could start them paused on Fast with the notice up front. Small, only worth it if
  `software_render_notice` shows more than a couple of players a month.
- `perf_sample` doesn't say whether the hint was shown; adding `battery_hint`/`software` flags would
  let the next review compare FPS before and after a hint in one query.
