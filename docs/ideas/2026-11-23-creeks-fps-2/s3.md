## Done

- **Creek heads narrow into their end** (`js/maps/water-shape.js`, `shoreHeights`). A creek's dead end
  used to stop in a square cut halfway across its last tile, with the water at full width right up
  to it. Now the last tile counts as half water in the smoothed outline, and the banks right round it
  may be raised a little (as a pond's are, never closing a hollow), so the water narrows into the
  end. Measured on 16 ends (dug ones on five maps plus the generated ones): the end tile's width
  against the creek's width three tiles back, median 0.73 → 0.50, worst 0.93 → 0.73. One side of an
  end stays straighter than the other where the ground mesh's diagonal runs (corner heights alone
  can't draw a point). Creek *mouths* (where a creek meets a pond or the river) were left alone: the
  water joins there, there's no stub.
  - Generated maps change only where they have creek ends: Chinandega and Sumatra (a few corners each).

- **Beaver ponds: measured, no shore pass needed** (changed from the brief). I built the monthly
  shore pass (shaping each pond to its full level, only when its dam rose or went, so it couldn't
  creep with the seasons) and measured it on 13 dam sites: their drawn shores were already smooth

  moved, no sharp bend, the same shore), so there's no corner to round. `water-shape-check` now holds

## Open questions for Cole

   The cause is how that water is drawn: each row of it is a flat sheet one step lower than the last,
   and the higher row's water spills sideways over the low ground west of it. Fixing it means drawing
   that water as one sloping sheet along the creek, a drawing change that also means refilming the
   intro's first shot. *Recommended: yes, as its own small session next time the intro is refilmed
   anyway; it's only seen in the first shot and from the north edge.*

   Exempting them needs a Sumatra-only rule. *Recommended: leave it; the difference is inside one
   tile and reads as silt.*
