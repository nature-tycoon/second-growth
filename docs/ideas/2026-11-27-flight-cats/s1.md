# Session 1: Amazon water animals (ideas 6, 7)

## Done

- **Caiman forelimbs on the ground.** The cause was the walk, not the model: every leg swung about the body's side axis, which for a leg sticking out sideways lifts the forefoot almost to hip height on the forward stroke and sinks it 0.02 body lengths into the ground on the back stroke. Sprawlers (`mo.sprawl`, the caiman and the Nile monitor, which shares the build) now turn each leg about the hip's upright, as a crocodile's upper arm does, the foot lifted only on the forward stroke. Measured over a stride: the forefoot stays on the ground through the whole stance and lifts at most 0.03 body lengths (was up to 0.05, near the hip at 0.06, and down to -0.02). Picture: the review page's walk pose.
- **Swimming tucks the legs.** Before, a swimming caiman strode in the water like on land. Now a sprawler in the water lays its legs back along its flanks and swims with its tail (`sprawlSwim`, eased in and out).
- **A followed fish shows through the water.** If the followed animal is completely under the surface, the tracking lens draws it faintly through the water, as it does through leaves, and fainter the deeper it is. This works for any fish or diving animal on any map. Nothing changes for an animal that breaks the surface (a swimming caiman, a boto coming up to breathe, a duck), so the November "swims on top of the water" fault stays fixed. The lens waits 0.4 s before it switches and then eases in, so a fish at the surface doesn't flicker. No hole is cut in the water, and the water is as murky as before. Checked in the game on the Amazon map: a followed red-bellied piranha shows faintly with the lens and is invisible without it.
- Records: 0040's water rule and 0018 (sprawlers). Checks: new cases in `canopy-support-check` (wholly under, breaking the surface, no flip in one frame).
