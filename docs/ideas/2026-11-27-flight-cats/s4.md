# Session 4: Interaction to Next Paint (idea 2)

Cole asked: "How do I improve interaction to next paint time?"

## The answer, short

**What INP is.** The time from a click, tap or key press until the screen shows the result. The browser measures every press in a visit and reports about the slowest (it skips one in 50). Google calls under 200 ms good and over 500 ms poor.

**Where we stand.** PostHog already records it (`$web_vitals`). Over the last 30 days the p75 (three visits in four are faster) is:

| | p75 | p50 | p90 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Desktop, secondgrowth.earth (462 visits) | 606 ms | 256 | 1173 |
| Desktop, itch.io (98) | 644 | 316 | 1685 |
| Phones (75) | 560 | 264 | 2861 |

By map: Sumatra 1632, reef 706, Atlanta 656, Chinandega 624, Amazon 568, Serengeti 504, PNW 494. That's "poor" on most maps.

**What drives it: the graphics chip, not our buttons.** A button's own code takes 0–2 ms (the field guide was the exception). The game redraws the 3D view all the time, and a click's change can only reach the screen together with the 3D picture. So a click waits for the 3D frame in progress, and then for every 3D frame the graphics chip has queued. Players' own numbers (`perf_sample`) fit this: half run under 27 fps and a quarter under 16. At 16 fps, waiting behind two or three queued frames is already 150–250 ms. Rarer long jobs (portraits drawn in the background, the field guide's first opening) push the worst press of a visit, which is what INP reports, to 600 ms and more.

**How to improve it**, in order of payoff:
1. Make frames cheaper on weak chips. This is the same work as frame rate (the performance reviews' list), and it lowers INP everywhere.
2. Keep the graphics chip's queue short, so a click doesn't wait behind several frames. Built as an A/B, see below.
3. Never do long work right after a press, and split big jobs into small ones. Done for the field guide and the background portrait warm-up.
4. Measure by interaction, not by page. Now reported, see below.

## Done

- **Found the real numbers** (above) with the PostHog connection (it works). `$web_vitals` gives one INP per visit and never says which press it was.
- **Reproduced it locally** with a new probe, `tools/inp-probe.mjs`. It clicks the real controls as real input, so Chrome measures each press the way players' INP is measured. It splits the time into waiting for the frame in progress, our code, and waiting for the paint, and names the scripts running meanwhile.
  - On this Mac (Apple M5, PNW, Fast, a grown farm), every interaction was 32–64 ms p75, nearly all of it the frame. The heaviest code was the field guide (17 ms, 30 ms with the CPU slowed 4x).
  - CPU throttling barely slowed the M5's frames, so it can't stand in for a weak laptop. Software drawing (the graphics work done on the CPU, 3–5 fps) can: there every click took **0.9–2.1 s**, almost all of it waiting for the paint behind queued 3D frames, while our code took 0–1 ms.
- **Field guide opens without drawing its pictures first.** Opened before the portraits were drawn in the background, it drew 30–60 of them (each a 3D render read back from the graphics chip) before it could show. It now shows at once with blank pictures, which fill in one at a time after it paints, never within 400 ms of a press (`thumbSrc`, `fillThumbs` in `js/ui/thumbnails.js`). Amazon's guide opened in the first minute: **320–376 ms → 32–48 ms** (its code 307–365 ms → 8–9 ms).
- **The background portrait warm-up waits for a lull.** Each portrait drawn in the background ran as a single job of up to 440 ms (software drawing) at forced moments during play, and a press landing on one waited for it. It now waits until 3 s after the last press.
- **The 3D draw can be held to two frames queued on the graphics chip, and the queue empties after a press so the press paints next** (`js/render3d/gpu-pacing.js`). It ships as an **A/B: half of sessions**, because I couldn't prove the gain here (see the open questions). With one frame queued, clicks painted in 16 ms but the M5 lost a third of its frames (60 → 40–46 fps), so that was dropped. With two, the M5 keeps 59 fps. Under software drawing, one run gave the panels 16 ms (from 1–2 s), but later runs with pacing on gave 0.7–2 s again. A software frame is one block of 300–400 ms, so where a click lands in it decides the wait, and that blurs any gain. Safeguards:
  - Frames that only waited are left out of the processor-time numbers, so they can't push automatic quality up.
  - Fences that never finish turn pacing off for the session.
  - A lost graphics context drops the pacing.
  - WebGL1 has no fences and isn't paced.
- **Tried and dropped:** making the frame after a press skip its work. It gave no measurable gain, because the queue is on the graphics chip, not in our frame.
- **INP is now reported by interaction** (`js/interaction-latency.js`):
  - `slow_interaction` (300 ms or more, up to 6 a visit) names what was touched (`stat-species`, `btn-goals`, `toolbar:cat=plant`, `map:plant`, `key:space`), with the three parts of the wait, the speed, the open panel and the fps.
  - `perf_sample` adds `inp_ms`, `interactions`, the slowest press since the last sample and `draws_waited_pct`.
  - `draw_pacing` rides on every event, so the A/B shows straight in `$web_vitals`.
  - Labels come only from the game's own markup. They never include page text or typed keys: a key in a text box is `key:text`.
- After, on the M5 (PNW, Fast, grown, pacing on): speed, pause, goals 32 → 16 ms; menu, settings, close, report, tool category, inspect, watch 40–64 → 32 ms; field guide 64 → 32; brush 48 → 48; time-lapse 32 → 32.
- **Checks:**
  - New: `gpu-pacing-check` (two in flight, one after a press, a slow chip stays paced, fences that never finish turn it off, WebGL1 untouched) and `interaction-latency-check` (labels and privacy, INP math, cap).
  - Passed: the headless suite (156 scripts, including perf-guard and species-name-count), analytics-privacy-check, perf-analytics-browser-check. The regression review's findings were fixed: stale fences after a lost context, waited frames in the CPU stats, key repeats, thumbnail fills next to presses, and browsers whose fences never finish.

## Open questions for Cole

1. **After two weeks, keep the draw pacing for everyone or remove it?** Compare p75 `$web_vitals_INP_value` and `perf_sample.fps` between `draw_pacing` true and false (desktop and phones apart). **Recommend:** keep it if INP drops by 15% or more and fps drops by under 3%. Otherwise remove it. I can run that query when you say.
2. **Speed and pause buttons were still slow under software drawing (1.5–2 s, the same as before)**, while the panels sometimes weren't. On the M5 they're 16 ms. **Recommend:** wait for `slow_interaction` data from real players before digging. If `speed=` labels top the list, look at what the first frames at a new speed draw.
3. **The field guide still recomputes every species' habitat when it opens** (8–17 ms on the M5, maybe 50–100 ms on a weak laptop). It could use the day's figures and show a "today's habitat" note. **Recommend:** leave it until `slow_interaction` shows `stat-species` or `key:g` near the top.
4. **The A/B decides per visit at random.** A player can get pacing in one visit and not the next. **Recommend:** fine for an A/B. Make it per browser only if the halves look too noisy.

## New ideas noticed

- Sumatra's INP p75 is 1.6 s, three times PNW's. It's also the map with the known slow opening view on the GPU (0038's open item). The two are probably the same problem, worth looking at together.
- The probe can't open the Journal or the Trees button at 1440×900 because the top bar folds them away (`fitTopbar`). Players at that size can't see them either. Check that the fold leaves them reachable.
- `perf_sample` shows players' drawing work at p95 38 ms (p75 of players). That is the per-frame budget's biggest lever for both frame rate and INP. The ranked list in the 2026-11-26 performance review applies directly.
- Clicks during the first minute are hit by the warm-ups: models, shaders and portraits. A first-minute `slow_interaction` breakdown would show whether the opening needs its own fix.
