# Session 3: Flood water look (idea 5)

## Done

- Found why floods looked blotchy. It was the drawing, not the simulation's flood pattern. The flood used the river's water shader, and three parts of it broke the sheet into blobs a tile or two across:
  - a "silt plume" noise mixed it toward a fixed olive colour, so it read tan and olive-green in patches (the average flood colour was olive, not brown);
  - a depth tint that stepped at each tile corner and went dark blue-green over the buried creek, leaving a dark band down the middle;
  - pale sky-reflection patches.
  Its edge also ended in hard cuts along tile lines.
- Flood water is now one even sheet of muddy brown with only a broad, slow drift of silt. It thins to nothing at a soft waterline that follows the land, with a faint scum line instead of surf. Ripples, downstream streaks, rain rings and cloud shadows stay. The flood's shader already had its own program, so the `FLOOD` switch adds no new one. The pond and river water are unchanged.
- The water depth now comes from a smooth share of wet ground nearby, so the edge is rounded. Each flood tile gets a centre point, so the usual creek flood, one tile wide on each bank, still shows water (the plan reviewer caught that thin strips would otherwise vanish).
- Measured at the game's camera angle on a seeded big flood (pnw, atlanta, amazon). The colour step between neighbouring flooded tiles fell from 6.8–7.5 to 4.3–4.6 (of 255). Big tile-to-tile jumps (over 12) fell from 16–19% of neighbours to 4–5%. Rebuilding the flood on a day it changes takes 6.6 ms instead of 5.8. The flood mesh has twice the triangles, about 7,000 on PNW's biggest flood, and only while a flood stands.
- `tools/flood-review.html` can now be pointed at chosen tiles (`setSpots`) and exposes `window.view` for measuring.
- `water-check` searched for flood round each vertex as if every vertex were a whole-number corner. It now counts whole tiles round the new centre vertices, and checks the river surface at corners only. The diff review also caught two bugs, now fixed: a centre's depth was measured from the wrong ground height, and on tiles part flowing and part still a centre's current took still corners in its mean.
- Checks pass: impact --run (13), water-check, water-shape, riverbank, terrain-patch, offmap-creek, flood-reach, perf-guard. Not looked at in the full game scene (only `flood-review.html`'s lit scene); no browser check covers floods.
- The rule is recorded in 0034.

## Open questions for Cole

1. Should floodwater stay brown everywhere, or carry a map tint, for example redder on Atlanta's clay or darker in the Amazon? **Recommend:** keep one brown for now. A map can already set `look.flood` if one looks wrong next to its soil.
2. Should a flood's edge show a faint line of foam and debris, or none? It now shows a faint one (35% of a pond's). **Recommend:** keep it faint. Real floods leave a scum line of foam and twigs at the edge.

## New ideas noticed

- Floods along creeks are mostly one tile wide on each bank (PNW rows 9–39). A real creek flood spreads wider over flat ground and stays narrow in a cut. `floodReach` could follow the land's height above the creek more closely. That is a simulation change and was not touched here.
- On every map, the sim's flood leaves a few dry knolls and spurs: 0–31 dry tiles with three wet sides, which reads as realistic high ground. Some are roads or buildings that can't flood. Left as they are.
- Seen from straight overhead, the water's sun glints are strong and look patterned. The game camera never looks straight down, so this was left alone.
