# Session 6: Eye-level foliage occlusion

## Done

- **Animals no longer pop in front of a bush at eye level.** Found two causes, both in the tracking
  lens (the thing that lets you keep seeing the animal you follow through cover, decision 0040).
  Neither came from the plants or the impostor cards.
  - **A hole through the bush.** At eye level the camera rides about 1.3 tiles behind a rabbit (more
    for bigger animals). The lens let you see through cover only within about the animal's own size,
    and cut a hole through anything farther in front. So a bush half a tile in front of the rabbit got a
    rabbit-shaped hole, and the rabbit, and any other animal standing beside it, showed fully, as if in
    front of the leaves. Measured in a staged scene: 80% of the followed rabbit's hidden parts and 61%
    of a second rabbit's showed at full strength. Now, at eye level, cover over the 75% of the way
    nearest the animal is seen through faintly instead, and only what is close to the lens (a leaf
    right at the camera) still opens. The second rabbit is now fully hidden by the bush.
  - **The faint drawing wasn't faint.** The lens draws the followed animal a second time at 42% so
    it shows through what hides it. In the real game that second drawing came out at 100%: three.js
    kept the animal's "opaque" shader, which ignores transparency, so the followed animal was drawn
    whole over every leaf and grass blade close in front of it, from above too. Now it is really 42%
    (measured in the game: 41–42% behind a leaf).
  - The followed animal still shows faintly through the bush, as 0040 intends; nothing else does.
  - Before/after stills taken in the game (PNW, eye level, a rabbit and a deer behind huckleberry
    and salal, each with a third shot with the lens off for comparison):
    `tools/release-output/eye-occlusion/` (`*-1-before.jpg`, `*-2-after.jpg`, `*-nolens.jpg`; not
    committed, release-output is ignored).
- `lens-depth-browser-check` now fails if the lens opens the bush in front of an animal at eye
  level (it used to open 2–8% of the view there; now 0%). Decision 0040's rules and history are updated.
- Checks: lens-depth, canopy-reveal and moment-camera browser checks, canopy-support-check,
  watch-check, perf-guard-check (results in the commit message).

## Open questions for Cole

1. **How strong should the followed animal show through cover?** It's now really at 42% for the
   first time (it was accidentally 100% in the game). From above, a selected deer under trees will
   now look fainter where leaves cover it than you're used to. *Recommended:* keep 42% and see how it
   feels; if it's too hard to find a selected animal from above, raise it for the overhead view only
   (one number, `LOOK_ALPHA`).
2. **Should the followed animal show through cover at all at eye level?** At eye level you're right
   behind it, so you could argue it should simply disappear behind a bush like any other animal.
   *Recommended:* keep the faint view: watch mode loses its subject otherwise, and a faint shape
   reads as "behind the bush", which a solid one didn't.

## New ideas noticed

- The see-through depth at eye level is capped at 2.5 tiles because the lens only knows the trunks
  within 3 tiles of the animal. For an elephant or giraffe (eye 6–9 tiles back) a bush more than 2.5
  tiles in front still opens, so an animal behind it near the big one can still show. Widening the
  trunk search would let the cap go.
- The headless lens check couldn't catch the 100% bug (its renderer happened to pick the transparent
  shader anyway); only a probe in the real game did. A small in-game probe in a browser check
  (pixel behind a leaf against the same view without plants) would guard it.
