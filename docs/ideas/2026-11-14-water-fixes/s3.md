# Session 3: Terrain tools

## Done

- **Fill & grade leaves flat ground.** A stroke now levels every tile it touches to one height: the
  height of the tile where the stroke starts (the brush's centre). Dragged across a slope it leaves a
  flat pad, cut into the hill above and built up below, with a one-tile slope down to the land round it.
  It now works on any land, not only water, mud and roads; plants on graded land stay, as with Raise and
  Lower ground. Ground already level costs nothing.
  - I used the start height rather than the brushed area's average: you can see it before you click,
    and a second stroke started on the pad continues it at the same level (a terrace).
  - Land more than 3.6 m (1.5 levels) above or below the stroke's level is skipped, so the pad stops
    where the slope gets too steep instead of leaving a cliff; a one-time hint says so.
  - Water: grading never moves a creek's or the river's bed, other water or a building's ground (the
    0048 rule). Filling water stands the ground at least a low bank (0.15 levels, ~35 cm) above that
    water's surface, so the filled ground is dry. A pond or marsh filled at its edge raises the corners
    it shares with the rest of it (never lowers them): without that, the filled shore tile sloped
    under the water and the water model turned it straight back into marsh. Ground graded below a
    pond's level beside it floods, as the Hollis water model expects.
  - The old `raiseToSurroundings` (which also raised the corners of the water beside a filled tile) is
    gone. Code: `gradeLevel`, `gradeTile`, `GRADE_REACH` in `js/maps/landform.js`; the fill tool in
    `js/tools.js`.
- **Gravel lots go with Demolish.** Demolish takes up gravel people laid (farmyards, pull-outs, Atlanta's
  riprap) to bare soil for $15 a tile, a road's price; natural gravel (beaches, bars, kopjes, reef
  rubble, the Serengeti crust) stays. Fill & grade levels gravel but keeps it; a one-time hint points to
  Demolish. Undo puts the lot back. Tool texts updated (Spanish too), and 0048's rules.
- Checks: `tool-actions-check` (new: a stroke levels every map's steepest dry patch to its start height;
  grading beside a creek never moves its bed; a filled pond tile stands dry; Demolish and gravel lots on
  every map, with Undo), `dug-creek-check`, `hollis-water-check`, `fish-water-check`,
  `terrain-relief-check`, `water-check`, `tool-actions-browser-check`, `perf-guard-check` all pass;
  headless suite: everything passes except `motion-sweep-check` (PNW coho spin 2.3 per 1000 frames, limit
  2), which fails the same on the commit before this session (1cbd18d), so it isn't from these changes.
  Looked at a before/after screenshot of a stroke on a PNW field slope: the furrows
  bend round a flat patch where it cuts the slope.

## Open questions for Cole

1. **Should Fill & grade still rip up roads?** It still turns a farm road into bare soil (as before),
   while gravel lots now need Demolish, which also takes up roads. *Recommended:* make roads
   Demolish-only too, so Fill & grade only shapes the ground and fills water, and "taking out what
   people built" is always Demolish.
2. **Should grading strip the plants and turn pasture or a plowed field into bare soil?** A real grader
   scrapes the sod; here graded land keeps its plants and its kind (a field keeps its furrows), as Raise
   and Lower ground do. *Recommended:* keep it as it is: a slip of the brush across a meadow shouldn't
   wipe it out, and Loosen soil already turns sod to bare soil.
3. **Start height or the brushed area's average?** I chose the start height (predictable, lets you
   build terraces). *Recommended:* keep it; an average shifts with every brush dab.
4. **How steep a pad edge is allowed?** The pad stops 3.6 m above or below its level, so its edge is at
   most a 24° bank over one tile. *Recommended:* keep 3.6 m; lower it if Cole sees banks that look too
   sharp.
