# Session 2: water model audit, connected ponds, salmon passage

Decision record: [0048](../../decisions/0048-water-and-elevation-model.md) (status: proposed).

## Done

- **Audit and rework proposal** (0048). Every recent water or elevation bug falls into five classes:
  edits leak into neighbouring water (corners are shared and nobody owns them), water bodies don't know
  about each other (creeks are fixed per tile, ponds are closed tanks, and only Hollis has tanks),
  player edits skip the generator's land rules, caps and noise stand in for missing physics, and the
  per-tile staircase on creeks. Proposed fix: every height corner gets one owner (land, a creek
  channel or a pond body), all terrain tools go through one edit path, creeks become graded channels
  with a seasonal level, and the Hollis pond model runs on every map. Four phases, each shippable;
  phase 1 is this session.
- **Salmon pass between a dug creek and the main creek, both ways.** Two causes. (1) The creek tool cut
  each tile from its own highest corner, so a creek dug across a slope was flat pools joined by dry
  chutes over a metre high. (2) Digging the tile that joins the main creek cut half a level *under* the
  main creek and dried two of its tiles, which blocked the main creek itself. Now digging never lowers
  another water body's bed, and a dug creek is graded toward the water it joins so its water covers
  the whole bed. Before: 0 of 6 salmon left a test creek in 60 days. After: 3 of 3 in each of three
  run seeds, and fish from the main creek swim up into it.
- **A pond dug up to the creek holds the creek's level.** It's dug to pond depth under the creek's water
  and, in the Hollis water model, stays at that level all summer (before: it fell 0.65 m below the
  creek). A pond even one tile away is still a vernal pool and draws down. Its tile note says it's
  joined to the creek.
- New check `tools/dug-creek-check.mjs` (fails on the old code). Water, beaver, fish, terrain, tool, perf
  guard and species-name checks pass.
- Balance: see the table at the end.

## Open questions for Cole

1. **Approve the rework plan in 0048?** It's 3 more phases (one edit path for all terrain tools; graded
   creeks with seasonal low water; the pond model on every map). *Recommended: yes, phase 2 first (one
   edit path), because it closes the "edit beside water breaks the water" bugs for Raise/Lower and
   Fill & grade too, and it's the smallest.*
2. **Should creeks run low in a dry summer?** Right now a creek is the same all year, so a pond joined
   to it never drops. Real Cascade-foothill creeks drop a lot in August. *Recommended: yes, in phase 3,
   gently (a hand's depth), so joined ponds and fish feel the dry season without stranding salmon.*
3. **Ponds on other maps.** Only Hollis has seasonal ponds. Serengeti charcos and Chinandega pools would
   really dry out in the dry season. *Recommended: yes for Serengeti and Chinandega in phase 4; Amazon,
   Sumatra and Atlanta stay wet.*
4. **A creek dug straight up a steep hill** keeps a few cascades (grading won't cut a ravine more than
   about 1.4 m deep). Should salmon leap a cascade, as real coho leap up to about 2 m? *Recommended: yes,
   in phase 3, with a leap height per species (coho high, cutthroat low).*

## New ideas noticed

- Existing motion-sweep failure, not from this session (identical without these changes): coho
  `spin` 2.3 per 1000 frames on pnw (limit 2). Worth a look in session 5 or a fish session.
- On a fresh farm coho rate the unshaded creek as no habitat at all (suitability 0), so a salmon placed
  there never moves. That's by design (shade first), but a player who digs a creek and watches it may
  think the fish are broken. A tile note ("needs shade") could say why.
- Raise/Lower ground on a tile beside a creek still moves the creek's corners (class A). Phase 2 fixes it.
- None of the Hollis water tile notes (the new "joined to the creek" one included) go through the
  translation layer. Fine while Hollis is English only.

## Balance (`calibrate pnw 15 5`, HEAD vs this change)

The scripted restoration digs no pond or creek. It changed only because its marsh, dug tile by tile on
a slope, no longer cuts into the marsh tiles it was already dug beside (83 corners, about 5 cm
shallower on average, at most 17 cm). Small land changes ripple through a chaotic ecology:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| score (range over 5 seeds) | 64 (59-70) | 64 (61-71) |
| native / water / food web | .42 / .59 / 1 | .47 / .62 / .9 |
| invasives | 24% | 21% |
| large herbivores | 62 kg/ha | 74 kg/ha |
| shrub tiles | 2535 | 2765 |

No vital sign fell meaningfully and no finding came or went except the herbivores, already over the
reference before. The 3-seed run also showed squirrels absent in the median (0 of K 5), which the
5-seed run doesn't.
