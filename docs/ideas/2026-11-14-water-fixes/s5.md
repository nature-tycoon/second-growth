# Session 5: Hunts and soaring

## Done

- **Snakes swallow their prey whole.** You were right: an anaconda's capybara kill went down the same
  path as a lion's, a carcass on the ground that it tore at and then left for scavengers. Now every
  snake (it's a trait, `swallowsWhole`, so the anaconda, python, boa, rat snake and garter snake all
  get it) leaves no carcass. The meal plays in two parts:
  - the first third, the prey lies on its side at the snake's jaws (held while it stops breathing);
  - then it goes in head first: drawn sliding into the mouth and shrinking away while a bulge grows
    behind the snake's head.
  - Afterwards, while the snake lies up digesting (5 game days, as before), the bulge moves down to
    the middle of its body and goes down. A capybara makes an anaconda about two and a half times as
    thick there; a vole in a garter snake a little less in proportion.
  - The meal lasts as long as before and the catch odds are unchanged, so kills per year stay where
    they were tuned. The only ecological change is that scavengers no longer get snake kills.
  - Seen on the close-up review page: `tools/animal-closeup-review.html?species=amazon:anaconda&gulp=210.8`
    (a meal just swallowed) and `&gulp=330.5` (half digested).
- **Soaring birds no longer fly backwards.** Found it: at watch pace a soaring hawk's step is about
  0.018 tiles, and the steering only turns an animal's heading for a target over 0.02 tiles away.
  The soar aimed at a point just one step ahead, so the heading froze while the bird went on round
  its circle: on PNW at watch pace, 369 of 2108 soaring frames had the hawk flying tail first. At
  normal speed it never happened, which is why the motion check never caught it. Now a soaring bird
  aims a little way round its circle ahead (about 20°), so it always faces along its turn. After:
  no tail-first frames in the simulation; 4 single drawn frames in 4393 (a turn blending), never
  held. Same fix for a hawk's "circle" over its prey before the stoop. Covers every soaring bird
  (red-tailed and red-shouldered hawks, white-backed vulture), and the bank still leans into the turn
  (checked: the wing on the inside of the circle is the low one).
- **Reef seabirds no longer fly backwards either.** The new check found noddies (and now and then a
  booby) flying tail first at normal speed while circling over a fishing spot, for the same kind of
  reason. Fixed the same way.
- Checks added: `bird-behavior-check` tests heading against flight for soaring hawks and vultures at
  normal and watch pace (it fails on the old code); `motion-sweep-check` has a new `tailFirst` count
  for every flier (limit 0) and a `SPEED=4` setting to run the sweep at watch pace (its distance
  limits now use the animals' own clock, which runs faster than the calendar at watch pace);
  `animal-life-check` and `snake-model-check` cover the swallow and the bulge.
- Checks run: bird-behavior, animal-life, snake-model, hunt-check (within limits), species-name-count,
  carcass-decay, scavenger-arrival, snake-hunt-probe, moments, animal-locate, challenging-ecology,
  motion-sweep (all maps, and at watch pace on PNW, Atlanta and Serengeti), seeded balance (below).
- Seeded balance (`calibrate`, 15 years, before vs after): all maps on 3 seeds, then PNW and Amazon on

## Open questions for Cole

1. **Should the coils show?** The constriction is only implied: the prey lies at the snake's jaws for
   the first third of the meal, but the snake's body doesn't wrap round it, because the snake model
   has one fixed resting curve. Drawing real coils would mean a new pose for the snake's body (a
   tight spiral around the prey), a medium-sized drawing job. *Recommended: wait until you've seen
   the swallow and bulge in the game; only do coils if the first part of the meal reads as the snake
   just lying next to its prey.*
2. **Should the bulge last longer?** A real anaconda digests a capybara for a week or more, and the
   bulge stays visible for days. In the game it fades over the snake's 5-day rest, which is a few
   seconds at normal speed and under a minute at watch pace. Making it last longer would be drawing
   only, but a snake that "still has a lump in it" while out hunting again would look odd.
   *Recommended: keep it tied to the rest as it is.*

3. **The reef's coral came out a little lower after the seabird fix** (coral .88 to .84, old coral .87
   to .81, median of 9 seeds; the score is 91 either way). The fix only changes the path noddies and
   boobies fly while circling over a fishing spot, which can change how often they catch reef fish.
   It's within the run-to-run spread, but it showed on both 6 and 9 seeds. *Recommended: keep the fix
   (birds flying backwards is the visible bug) and watch the reef's coral in the next balance pass.*

## New ideas noticed

- Other "carrot one step ahead" steering may freeze headings at watch pace the same way (any behaviour
  whose target moves along with the animal). The new `tailFirst` count only checks fliers;
  `SPEED=4 node tools/motion-sweep-check.mjs` at watch pace on every map would be a cheap sweep to
  find others.
- At watch pace (`SPEED=4 DAYS=2 node tools/motion-sweep-check.mjs`) every limit holds except the
  Amazon river dolphin swimming sideways (7.6 per 1000, limit 4). At normal speed it is already a known
  "heading shivers" glitch; probably the same cause. Not looked into.
- A python or boa that has just eaten could look for cover (under a log or in thick shrub) to digest,
  as real ones do, instead of lying where it caught its prey.
