# Session 4: Beaver dam polish

## Done

- **Dams rise over the years** (`b.rise` in `js/sim/hollis-water.js`, raised in `damDay` in `js/sim/wildlife-beavers.js`).
  - A new dam starts low: it backs the creek up about 30 cm over its own water, just enough to still it into a small pond (13 tiles at the Hollis test site, against 29 at full height).
  - While the colony tends it, beavers raise it a step on the first day of each month from June to November. It reaches about half height in its first season and full height in its second. The pond deepens and spreads as it rises, and the dam line lengthens across the valley.
  - A flood that breaches a tended dam now knocks it down a step; the beavers raise it again in the building months. An untended dam still washes out, as before.
  - A dam built over the draining pool of an old one starts low too.
  - **Changed from the plan:** I first had dams take about three years to reach full height. In the 15-year beaver soak most colonies left (willow eaten out) before their pond was more than marsh, and beavers fell from 4–6 to 2–4. At two seasons, beavers stay at 5–6 and dam ponds peak higher than before (69 tiles against 44–50), though the farm's total pond peak on that seed is a little lower (93 against 110).
- **Save/load:** how far a dam is built is saved. A save from before this change loads its dams at full height. Checked both.
- **Trees at the dam's ends** (`fellAtDam`): when a dam is built, and each time it is raised, the woody plants on the bank tiles where its ends land are cut. Willow and dogwood are cut back to resprout (they are the beavers' food). A big conifer comes down as a log along the bank. The sapling waiting under a tree goes too, or it would have grown up through the mound.
- **Spill over a full dam** (drawing only, `damSpill` in `js/render3d/terrain.js`): when the pond is full, a thin, fainter version of the waterfall's white curtain runs over the crest of the mound across the channel and down into the creek below. It's its own mesh, so the waterfalls are unchanged. It turns on and off about twice a year at the test site, so it doesn't redraw the water day to day.
- **New check** `tools/beaver-dam-growth-check.mjs` (7 cases): a new dam is low but still makes a pond within a month, and isn't spilling on day one; tended, it reaches full height in its second season and its pond grows; flood breach and rebuilding over a draining pool; save/load and old saves; felling, including the sapling, willow and big-tree cases; **the dam is drawn one day after it's built through the normal game loop**, and drawn taller after it is raised; the spill is drawn at the crest when full, faces the right way, and goes when the pond drops; how often the spill turns on and off in a year.
  - The s5 report of a dam showing only after a forced redraw did not reproduce here. The plant drawing now also redraws a dam's tile whenever its crest or line changes, as a safety net.

## Open questions for Cole

1. **Should the creek below a full dam run lower?** Unchanged from s5: the game doesn't track how much water a creek carries, and real dams leak and keep the creek flowing. The spill is now drawn, which shows the water going over.
   *Recommend: leave it.*
2. **Growth speed.** Dams now take about two building seasons to reach full height. Real dams keep growing for decades, but colonies here mostly last 2–5 years before their willow runs out, so anything slower meant ponds that never got big.
   *Recommend: keep two seasons. If you'd like dams to keep growing for longer, the better lever is colonies that last longer (more willow regrowth), which is a balance change for its own session.*
3. **Big trees at a dam's end come down as logs.** Real beavers usually don't fell big conifers; they build the dam against them. Drawing the mound around a standing trunk would need new drawing, so I laid the tree down as a log instead.
   *Recommend: keep the log.*

## New ideas noticed

- A full dam isn't raised again, so a sapling that grows up at a dam's end later is only cut after a flood breach. Rare and slow (trees take years), but a yearly check would close it.
- The spill is drawn only over mounds across the channel. A dam held lower by a bank spills round its end over that bank in the sim, and nothing is drawn there.
- Session 5 (waterfall look) can restyle the spill along with the falls: it uses the same curtain builder (`curtain` in terrain.js).
