# Session 7: American dipper

## Done

- **A new PNW bird, the American dipper** (*Cinclus mexicanus*). It arrives on its own by habitat rules and is never bought. It needs a clean creek, with the water quality that comes from shade and native banks, as the sign of the caddisfly and mayfly larvae it eats. It does best on fast water: riffles and the fall, read from how steeply the creek bed drops. Ponds, marshes and the river don't count, even on the same creek. A restored Hollis creek holds about one pair: in a 15-year restore the median is 1 bird, with room for 3.
- **How it behaves:**
  - It stands on rocks in the creek and bobs up and down about once a second.
  - It walks about in the riffle and goes under the water to feed, more briskly than a heron.
  - Now and then it flies low and straight along the creek to another rock 2–8 tiles off. A flight never starts unless its whole line stays over water or the bank.
  - It lands only on its creek or the bank beside it. That includes when a hawk scares it off.
  - In the nesting months (April–May) it stays close to the nearest waterfall, where real dippers nest beside or behind the falling water.
  - It flies under the alders, not up over the treetops.
- **Drawing:** it reuses the songbird body, which is cheap and instanced like the other small birds. It is plump and slate-grey with a grey-brown head and a short cocked tail, and it stands with its legs in the water. It has a field-guide entry and a website portrait. The homepage now says 410 species.
- **Built on what was there:** the heron's wading became a `wades` trait instead of a list of body kinds (herons and cranes behave exactly as before). The dipper adds `fastWater` (creek only) and `bobs`. No species-name checks were added.
- **Checks:** a new `dipper-check` tests that it keeps to its creek: 0% of standing time off the creek, and 6% of flight crossing land, all at bends. The motion sweep watched 4,000 dipper frames with no glitches. These pass: `calibrate pnw` (score 72, in range), `perf-guard`, `species-name-count`, `website-check`, and the checks `impact.mjs --run` named. Two checks had the species total hard-coded, and both now count 204. The regression reviewer's three points are fixed: the bob and dunk ease out on take-off instead of jumping, a dipper now reacts to hawks, and the whole-map search for a creek is skipped when no creek can hold dippers.
- **Not seen in a browser.** The bob, the dip under the water and the low flight are checked by numbers only. Nobody has looked at them on screen.

## New ideas noticed

- When a hawk passes, small birds dart straight away from it in any direction. A dipper now lands only on its creek, but its escape can still cut across a bend. Real dippers often dive under the water instead. A "dives to hide" escape for the dipper (and ducks) would look more real.
- A creek's water quality is mostly shade from the banks, so a sunny but clean, fast mountain creek scores low. A simple "fast water carries oxygen" bonus for steep reaches would help dippers and trout alike.
- The kingfisher, heron and dipper all wade or fish on the same creek but take no notice of each other. Spacing waders along the creek, with herons shooing smaller birds off, would add life cheaply.
