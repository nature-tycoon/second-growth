# Session 3: Pond edge cliff

## Done

- Found the cause. When you widen a pond that a creek runs into, the game re-levels the whole pond after every tile you dig. It measured the creek's bed from the creek's lowest corner. But the pond had just lowered the corners it shares with the creek, so every re-level found the creek a little lower and dug the pond and creek down again, about 19 cm per tile. A 6×4 widening sank the pond about 4.5 m: water far under the rim, a cliff all round.
- Fix (`js/terrain-edit.js`): the creek's bed is now read from the corners the pond doesn't share (`channelBed`), so re-levelling is stable and the creek keeps its bed. The bank round a creek-fed pond also stands 15 cm over the creek's water level. It used to be built for a plain pond's depth and could end up under the creek's water.
- In the test case, the widened pond now holds the creek's water level (0.40 before and after), its floor is a pond's depth under it, and the bank is dry. Before, the water went from 0.40 down to −1.5.
- New regression check in `tools/dug-creek-check.mjs` covers three cases: widening a pond beside the map's creek, one with a creek dug into it, and one wrapped round the creek's end tile. It checks the same surface, no pit, a rim over the water, the creek's bed unmoved, and that digging the stroke again changes nothing. A creek tile the pond wraps on every side sets no floor (`channelBed` gives null), so the ratchet can't come back there. It fails on the old code (water −0.87 → −2.79) and passes now. Decision 0048 is updated.

## Open questions for Cole

1. **Ponds already sunk in existing saves aren't repaired.** Recommended: leave them. Filling and re-digging the pond fixes it, and an automatic repair could reshape ponds people built on purpose.
2. **A pond widened down along a sloping creek settles at the creek's lowest joined point** (in the test, 19 cm lower). Water finds its outlet, so this is realistic. Recommended: keep it.
3. **Where a widened pond ends up dug a little deeper than the creek's level minus a pond's depth, the foot of its bank is a shallow sedge margin.** The rim stays 20–35 cm over the water. Recommended: keep it. It's realistic and nothing like the pit.
4. **A strip of land squeezed between the creek and the pond stays marshy**, because both of its edges are held by water. Recommended: keep it. It's a realistic wet margin, and filling it is one stroke.

## New ideas noticed

- Widening a pond uphill pulls the whole floor down to its lowest point, so the uphill edge becomes a steep one-tile bank. A gentler cut (the bank sloped over two tiles) would look more natural.
- A freshly dug pond on its own starts nearly empty and fills with rain. A short "fills with rain" hint on the dig tool might stop it reading as a bug.
- Rivers take the same code path but aren't in the new check. Adding a river case (a pond dug beside the PNW or Sumatra river, then widened) would close that.
- The Hollis model's `feedLevel` still reads all of a creek's corners, so on a creek running end-on into a pond the rim can stand about 23 cm over the water rather than 15. Harmless, but the two could share `channelBed`.
