# Session 2: Birds and fish motion

Decision record: [0039](../../decisions/0039-steady-flight-level-fish-floating-ducks.md). Drawing only; the simulation and ecology are unchanged.

## Done

- **Bird flight holds a steady line.** The cause was the height target, not the wing beat (the body doesn't move with the flap): a flier's drawn height was re-aimed every frame at the ground or the crowns just ahead, so it climbed over every crown and sank between them, was lifted out of a crown 0.4 tiles in one frame, and stopped dead at the top of each climb. Now a bird in level flight climbs when the way ahead needs it but holds its height, gliding down slowly only after 1.5 s of lower ground; climbs round off into level flight; it looks further ahead so climbs start earlier. PNW: jolt (99th percentile) 0.21 → 0.0225 tiles a frame, quick up-and-downs 7.3 → 0.4 a minute; Atlanta 0.098 → 0.0225 and 10.5 → 0.7. Fliers clip crowns no more than before.
- **Mallards float on the creek.** They floated at the tile's own water level; the drawn creek sheet steps down pool to pool and stands higher, so a duck could sit under it (486 of 7064 samples, up to 0.09 tiles deep). Every floater (ducks, beaver, otter, frogs, turtles…) now floats at the drawn surface, and on drawn water at the bank's edge where it's deep enough; otherwise it stands on the bank. Now 0 under. Checked at eye level in the browser: the mallard's waterline sits exactly on the creek.
- **Reef fish hold a level depth.** They kept to a fraction of the water over the seabed right under them, so they followed every rock; and a coral head coming into view lifted a big fish 0.3 tiles in one frame. Now they swim over a seabed smoothed 1.2 tiles round them, rising over coral and rock as a slope and settling back slowly. Jolt 0.036 → 0.010, quick bobs 3.1 → 2.0 a minute, biggest one-frame lift 0.3 → 0.02.
- **Follow camera.** Its height eases on a spring (never more than 0.25 tiles behind, in practice 0.21 at most following reef fish); across the ground it still follows exactly, so fast animals stay centred. A jump (a tree removed under an orangutan) is followed at once. Camera jolt over reef fish 0.0035, a third of the fish's own.
- New check `tools/motion-smoothness-check.mjs` (runs in the headless suite). `flight-canopy-check` tightened (jolt limit 0.4 → 0.05) with a 1 s take-off grace instead of 0.6 s; `animal-locate-check` updated for the eased camera height.
- No measurable CPU cost (`Actors.update` 0.05 ms reef, 0.08 ms PNW per frame in node, same as before).

## Open questions for Cole

1. **A held cruise height means a bird crossing from forest to a clearing stays up at treetop height for about 1.5 s before gliding down.** Recommended: keep it; that's how a real bird crosses a gap. If you see birds looking "stuck high" over meadows, the knobs are `HOLD` and `GLIDE` in `js/render3d/flight-clearance.js`.
2. **The eye-level camera under the sea still jumps up over seabed bumps right under the eye itself** (it never goes below the seabed). The fish are smoother now, so it should rarely show. Recommended: leave it unless you still see bumps at eye level on the reef; the fix would be the same smoothed seabed for the eye's floor.
3. **A floating duck's legs show through the clear creek water, hanging down.** Real dabbling ducks paddle with their legs under them, so that's right in principle, but they're drawn in a standing pose. Recommended: a small model change to tuck the legs back while afloat, as a later polish if it bothers you.

## New ideas noticed

- Dabbling: a duck afloat could tip forward head-under now and then (only head and front under, tail up), the behaviour Cole described. The float height is right now, so this is just a pose (`birdHead` for floaters) plus a timer.
- `animal-culling-check` fails on this branch before this session's changes too: a zebra coming back on screen expects its leg clock at `phase × π` (22.0) and gets 3.4 (`tools/animal-culling-check.mjs:89`), probably since the stride/leg-clock change (`st.legPhase`). Pre-existing, not touched here.
- The flight look-ahead (`canopyTop`) samples a 5-point cross every 0.7 tiles; with tall trees a precomputed "canopy height" grid blurred over two tiles would make both the lookup and the held height cheaper and steadier, if flier counts grow.
