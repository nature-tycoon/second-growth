# Session 3: depth-based lens hole

Decision record: [0040](../../decisions/0040-depth-based-lens-hole.md). Code: `js/render3d/focus.js`.
Check: `node tools/lens-depth-browser-check.mjs` (pictures in `tools/release-output/lens-depth-*.png`).
Before/after pictures: `s3-lens-*-before.png` / `-after.png` in the shared notes folder.

## Done

- Close round the tracked animal nothing is cut any more: the animal is drawn again faintly over
  everything, so it shows through the bush, crown, grass or trunk in front of it, and the plant it
  is in stays whole (`render3d/look-through.js`). Cole saw every cut tried there as its shape: box
  (rectangle), ellipsoid (oval), then a jagged circle. Pictures: `s3-lookthrough-*.png` in the
  shared notes folder.
- Only cover close round the animal (about its size) is seen through; a tree farther in front
  stays in front of it, and what lies farther than that opens over the animal's outline. Trunks
  are never cut. The extra opening is small and scales with the animal (was 0.85 to 1.45 tiles
  for every animal, now 0.06 to 0.3).
- The animal passes behind the trunks close in front of it instead of showing through them; the
  gap stays exactly on the animal (it trailed behind, so a mushroom it had passed stayed opened);
  no dark patch on trunks behind which a small animal walks.
- Foliage nearer the camera opens wider the nearer it is: a share of the way to the eye at eye
  level, of four window radii from above. It starts a quarter of the way toward the camera, full width from 80%.
- At eye level the gap is sized on screen, at the animal's depth, so leaves right at the lens no
  longer open a hole that fills the near view.
- A soft rim: over the outer third, leaves thin out by leaf-sized specks fixed in the world. A
  screen-pixel dither was tried first and looked grainy.
- Bushes in front of a small animal at eye level part like canopy. The old rules already did this
  (a floor plant stays whole only when it shows wholly below the animal's feet or to its side);
  the new check's rabbit behind a huckleberry shows 100% from four sides.
- A trunk that shows below the animal's feet on screen stays (it can't hide the animal); before it
  was cut off midway. Its limbs still open with the leaves.
- Measured from above (deer under big-leaf maples, four sides): the lens used to open 44 to 59% of
  the view, now 26 to 43%, and the deer shows 100% in both.
- Checks: `lens-depth-browser-check` (new), `follow-support-browser-check` (palm 83 to 90% kept,
  old 82 to 89%), `canopy-reveal-browser-check` (80 of 80), `canopy-support-check` (text match of
  the discard line updated), `animal-locate-check`: all pass.
- Cost: a few extra shader operations, one noise call only for fragments in the rim, only while an
  animal is selected. No new passes.

## Open questions for Cole

1. **An earlier lens rework is still unmerged: `claude/wave3-s5-tracker-lens-aaed29` (commit
   e0ac9e2, 2026-11-09).** It cuts an ellipsoid instead of the box and starts the canopy gap from
   the animal's outline. Its notes say you called a dithered soft edge "old school" then. This
   session's soft rim is not a screen dither: it thins the leaves by leaf-sized clumps fixed in the
   world (see `tools/release-output/lens-depth-canopy-*.png`). Both branches change the same lines
   of `focus.js`. *Recommended:* look at the pictures; if the clumpy rim reads well, keep this
   branch and close the wave3-s5 branch (its ellipsoid body cut could be ported later as a small
   change). If the rim still looks old school, set the rim width to 0 (`0.65`/`0.35` in `focus.js`
   become `1.0`/`0.0`) and keep the rest.
2. **How wide should near-camera foliage open?** It reaches the full window (about 1 to 1.5 tiles
   from above, 0.2 to 0.55 at eye level) at 70% of the way to the camera. *Recommended:* keep it,
   and tell me after watching a few animals in forest if the canopy should open wider or tighter;
   it's one number (`0.7` in `focus.js`).
3. **Should the soft rim be wider?** Now the outer third of the gap thins out. *Recommended:* keep a
   third. A wider rim reads softer but leaves more speckled leaves over the view.
4. **The trunks of the tree an animal stands under can still be cut where they rise past its feet on
   screen.** *Recommended:* leave it: a trunk that passes in front of the animal has to open there to
   show it, and keeping the stretch below its feet already makes the cut look like a gap, not a
   slice.

5. **How strongly should the animal show through cover?** Now 42% (`LOOK_ALPHA` in
   `look-through.js`). At 60% it looked as if it stood in front of the leaves. *Recommended:* keep
   42%; lower it if hidden animals look pasted on, raise it if they're hard to find.

## New ideas noticed

Cole asked to do these three (2026-11-11). Outcome:

- **See-through trees by the lens's leaf clumps: tried, not kept.** Leaving out whole leaf clumps
  instead of blending crowns at 28% does fix the out-of-order glass look, but each leaf mass is a
  closed shell drawn one side only, so cutting part of it shows its hollow inside: crescents and
  rings on bushes, crowns that read as eaten (`s3-see-through-*.png` in the shared notes folder). Logged in 0040. *Question for Cole:* is the glassy
  overlap in see-through mode a problem for you? *Recommended:* leave it; a clean fix means
  recording each leaf mass in the plant models so whole masses drop out, a bigger job for its own
  batch if you want it.
- **Finer close-up bush at eye level: not built, bigger than it looked.** Plant models are chosen
  per patch by zoom, not per plant, so a finer bush only next to the lens needs a new close-up tier
  through the plant pools, view culling and shadows (like the animals' close-up models, 0018).
  Making the existing close model finer would roughly quadruple bush triangles in every eye-level
  view, too much for the low-end target (0004). Also, everything else is low-poly, so a smooth bush
  may look out of place. *Question for Cole:* do the faceted leaves right at the lens bother you?
  *Recommended:* no change; the lens now cuts most of a bush that is that close.
- **Lens check through the real eye camera: done.** `lens-depth-browser-check` now drives
  `EyeCamera` itself, from four sides, swung half off the bush, and lifted to look down over it. The
  rabbit shows 100% in all eight views; looking down over the bush, the lens opens 0.1 to 0.5% of
  the view.
