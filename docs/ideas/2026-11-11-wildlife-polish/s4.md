## Done

- Compared Cole's movement model with fixing today's code. Only `monkey/canopy-rewrite-wip` holds unmerged
  work. The other branches (`monkey/canopy-walker`, `canopy-primates`, `smooth-primate-hitches`,
  `codex/primate-*`, `codex/canopy-climbing-fixes`) are fully in `main`. The rewrite is about 29,000 lines
  on a base 307 commits old, its own audit says "rollout blocked", and it was never shown to be cheaper.
  Chose piece-by-piece fixes. The branch stays as a reference for the crown-gap follow-up below.
- Measured today's problems with a new headless tool (real `game.update` + `Actors.update`, 8 of each
  primate, a patchy and a dense stand). The worst problem was speed: the drawn monkey trailed its
  simulated position by 1–2.7 tiles on average and was over a tile behind 39–77% of the time. While
  catching up, the body managed only 0.3–0.7 of the simulation's speed. That trailing is behind
  "sliding", sudden reroutes and snaps: the body keeps re-aiming at a point that has already moved on.
  Causes: the route along limbs is 1.5–2.2× the straight line (the code assumed 1.33), and walker
  monkeys are drawn at most 1.5 body lengths a second (siamangs one arm stride per swing), which
  capuchins' simulated speed already exceeded.
- Fix: a primate's simulated climbing speed is now its drawn top speed along the wood divided by the
  measured route length (`drawnClimbPace / CANOPY_ROUTE`). The stride and swing constants are shared
  with the drawing so they can't drift apart. At normal speed the drawn body now trails by 0.5–0.8
  tiles (orangutans aside), is over a tile behind 15–36% of the time, and keeps up at 0.73–0.85 of
  the simulation's speed. Primates travel about a third (capuchin, siamang) to two thirds (howler,
  langur) as fast as before, nearer real troops, which rest most of the day.
- At speed 2 and above, walker monkeys' strides and leaps now quicken with the game (up to 4×) instead
  of staying at real-time pace. That halved their trailing at speed 2. Apes keep real-time limbs (two
  existing checks require it).
- Orangutans reach 1.6 body lengths across a gap (their arm span) instead of 0.85. No measurable gain:
  most crossings fail the clearance check, not the reach (see open questions).
- Squirrels and sloths are unchanged (not measured), so the default Hollis balance run is identical.
- Checks: `canopy-grips`, `canopy-route`, `canopy-support`, `canopy-walker-profile`, `climber-cap`,
  `monkey-pace`, `monkey-transfer`, `orangutan-motion`, `primate-behavior`, `primate-stall`: all pass.
  `balance-sim restore 15` (seeded): identical before and after.
  Primate maps (Amazon, Sumatra, Chinandega forest fixtures, 9 years, three seeds): year-9 counts move
  both ways within seed noise. Over the three seeds, howlers were 26/27/21 before and 17/20/27 after,
  spiders 22/4/13 → 13/11/14, siamangs 13/11/17 → 6/14/12, langurs 26/20/14 → 16/18/19, and capuchins
  10/14/10 → 13/12/11. No species dies out or trends one way.
- `play-profile amazon high 1` (play and top-speed views), before → after: 60 fps both; simulation 0.4 → 0.4 ms
  and 0.7 → 0.7 ms; drawing 2.3 → 2.3 ms and 6.0 → 5.0 ms (animals 0.09 → 0.21 and 0.24 → 0.25 ms, which
  depends on how many are on screen). No cost added.
- Browser check (a grown Amazon game in real Chrome, normal speed, camera on a troop, 10 s): 23 howlers and
  spider monkeys, drawn body 0.32 tiles from its simulated spot on average (p95 0.58), none over a tile,
  no snaps, no browser errors.

## Done (follow-up: Cole said "do your recommendations" and asked about siamangs)

- **Siamangs no longer float between trees.** Before, a siamang crossed any gap in a straight line at
  climbing pace, hanging from nothing with its arms still swinging. Now it lets go and leaps a short
  arc in a third of a second to one second, its free arm held out ahead, and catches the far limb.
  It only leaps gaps a siamang could: up to 4 body lengths across, 1.6 up, 4 down.
- **The simulation knows which neighbouring crowns connect** (`sim/canopy-links.js`), estimated from
  the same data the trees are drawn from and fitted to 200 recorded crossings. Siamangs and orangutans
  only travel between crowns they can cross; walker monkeys always found a way, so they're unchanged.
- **Orangutans climb down and walk a gap** the crowns don't span, then climb the far tree. In a grown
  Sumatra forest that's 11% of their time on the ground; siamangs never come down.
- Orangutans: blocked 33% → 19% of the time (dense stand 36% → 21%), and they trail by half as much
  (1.42 → 0.73 tiles). Siamangs are now honestly blocked 6–17% where before they floated across.
- Checks: the 13 primate and related checks plus the new `canopy-links-check` all pass.
  `balance-sim restore 15` identical; Amazon and Chinandega identical; Sumatra within seed noise.
  Browser check on a grown Sumatra game: siamangs and langurs 0.19 tiles from their spot, no snaps, no errors.
- Deleted the local `monkey/canopy-rewrite-wip` branch. The copy on GitHub (`origin`) is untouched.

## Open questions for Cole

1. **Delete the rewrite branch on GitHub too?** I deleted it only locally; deleting it on GitHub is a
   push. *Recommended:* yes: `git push origin --delete monkey/canopy-rewrite-wip`. Everything worth
   keeping is described in 0041 and the commit stays reachable for 90 days.
2. **The orangutan's ground crossing is drawn as a quick ease from the limb to the ground**, not a
   hand-over-hand climb down the trunk. *Recommended:* fine for now; a proper trunk descent is a
   drawing job worth doing if you notice it.
3. **Some crossings are still blocked** (siamangs 6–17%, orangutans about 20% in mixed-growth test
   stands; far less in a grown forest). *Recommended:* leave it. Tightening the rule strands animals
   in one tree; loosening it brings back waiting at gaps.

## New ideas noticed

- Capuchins are on the move about 90% of the time and howlers about half. Real howlers rest about
  two-thirds of the day, and every species rests through the midday heat. A per-species activity budget
  (as hunters have, 0014) would look more natural and also cut how much the bodies have to chase.
- Capuchins and colobus still snap forward a few times a minute in a patchy stand (5–7 per 8 monkeys per
  40 s, unchanged). Worth tracing with the measuring tool.
