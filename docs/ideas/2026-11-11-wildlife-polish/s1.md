# Session 1: small animals

## Done
- **Garter snakes hunt and brumate properly.** Two bugs, both in `js/sim/`:
  - Garter snakes are semi-aquatic (`move: 'semi'`), and the rule that keeps caimans and otters hunting from the water's edge (no step more than 2 tiles from water, `huntBlocked`) applied to them too. Frogs out in the meadow were off limits, so a snake started about one hunt in fifty tries and went hundreds of days without a meal. A new species flag `huntsAshore` (set on the garter snake only) lifts that limit. Snakes now start a hunt on about a third of tries (304 vs 16 over 10 years, seed 7).
  - Brumation waited for a recent meal: a hunter only settled into winter shelter if it had eaten in the last week. That suits a bear but not a snake, and since snakes never ate, they stayed out all winter. Brumation now skips that check (bears and chipmunks keep it). Snakes now spend 30-60% of Dec-Feb brumating, with the short clear-day arousals 0009 already allows, and are active from March to October.
  - Recorded in decision 0009.
- **Brush rabbits establish.** The habitat already had room for 28 rabbits from year 2, but rabbits arrived one at a time (about one a year at Standard) and a lone rabbit can't breed. Each was usually caught before a second arrived, so the farm held 0-1 rabbits for seven years. They now arrive as one or two (`groupSize: [1, 2]`, the same as many small PNW species), so a pair can settle in the brush and breed. Habitat, capacity, home range and predators are unchanged.

## Open questions for Cole
- **I couldn't reproduce a garter snake that never moves.** In headless runs, in the real frame loop and in the drawn 3D view, snakes walked about 75% of the time in every warm month. The bugs above made them act wrong (always hungry, out all winter), but not stand still. Recommended: if you see it again, click the snake and send a screenshot of its status line (or the save). Its status says whether it is hunting, resting or in winter shelter.
- **Should cover protect rabbits at Standard difficulty?** Hiding in brush only lowers a predator's catch chance at Challenging. At Standard, a rabbit in a blackberry thicket is as easy to catch as one in the open. Recommended: leave it. It's shared across every map and would move many species, so it belongs in its own change if you want it.

## New ideas noticed
- Garter snakes still eat rarely: their only prey is the chorus frog, and they're slow ambushers. Real common garter snakes also eat slugs, earthworms and rough-skinned newts (they're one of the few animals that can handle the newt's toxin). Adding newts to their diet would be a nice story for the field guide, but it's a balance change.
- Brush rabbits only hide from predators at Challenging. A brush-rabbit "freeze then dash into the thicket" escape would look good and suit the species.
