## Done

- **One source.** `tools/intro-film.mjs` now stages the film from `tools/intro-film-plan.mjs`, the shot list you approved. The hand-drawn deer and cougar lines and the hand-set cameras are gone. `tools/intro-film-shots.mjs` derives everything else from the plan: the layers (one per shot), the time of day, the beats, `SUBJECTS`, `PREDATOR` and `SLOWMO`. The map page, F2's sight-line check, the motion check and the film all read the same paths and cameras. Only `SOUND` is still written by hand, and it is still timed for the old 40 s film. F4 retimes it.
- **Shots are layers.** There are 12 layers, joined by cuts, with three dissolves: from black into shot 1, today's farm into the restored farm mid-rise, and the rewind. Shot 10 has two layers: the rise filmed on the restored farm (`rise30`, the same camera as shot 9, as you approved), then the map glide dissolving over its end.
- **Animals** (in the plan, `animalAt`):
  - Each path is a smooth curve through the plan's marks.
  - The time along it is the marks' schedule, smoothed over about 0.3 s. Starts, stops and changes of pace ease, and no stretch goes faster than the plan's own average for that stretch.
  - Heading turns gradually from where an animal is facing to where it's going as it speeds up. Its gait follows its speed: a doe easing off from browsing gallops, and a cougar pulling up keeps running until it stands.
  - The doe's fence jump and the cougar's pounce are declared jumps, placed by distance along the path and drawn with the game's pounce arc.
  - The cougar stalks under 1 m/s and sprints for 2.8 s. The doe gallops at 12–12.8 m/s. The restored farm's elk walk at 0.7–1.2 m/s.
  - In the slow motion (17.75–19.25 s, half speed), the whole world's clock slows too: legs, wind and water.
- **Cameras** (in the plan, `cameraAt`):
  - Every move eases in and out, with speed and acceleration starting and ending at zero.
  - A moving camera's height is eased between its start and end heights, so it doesn't follow every bump in the ground.
  - Looks that follow animals are smoothed over 0.45 s.
  - The horizon stays level (roll 0.00° everywhere).
  - A 45-frame warm-up before each layer (staged and posed, not drawn) means a cut never shows a pose still settling.
- **Staging: animals pitched to the slope.** The game draws every walker level. So an animal facing up or down a slope has its uphill hooves in the ground and its downhill ones in the air. This was the remaining cause of "sinks into the ground": the old forest slopes down to the farm, and every animal in the film runs downhill. The film tilts its cast to the drawn ground, using a per-animal pitch the drawing already adds but that ground animals never set. This is film staging only. The game is unchanged (see question 1).
- **Bugs fixed in the checks:**
  - The probe recorded each species as a number. So the motion check judged every animal by its default limits, and the cougar's 1 m/s stalk limit was never applied. It now records the species' key.
  - Speeds straddling the edge of the slow motion were scaled by one end's rate, which gave false 15 m/s spikes. They're now measured over the real time they span.
  - A leaping cougar was judged by a gallop limit it doesn't have. It's now judged by its sprint limit.
  - The stalking crouch, which the drawing does by lowering the whole body (see question 2), is declared like a leap.

## Open questions for Cole

1. **Pitch walking animals to the slope in the game too?** The game draws every animal level. On a slope its uphill feet go into the ground and its downhill feet hang in the air. That's what you saw in the first cut, and it happens in play on every hillside. The film now tilts its own animals as staging. *Recommendation:* yes, as its own small change in the drawing: a pitch from the ground ahead and behind each walker, plus the motion sweep before and after. Then the film's staging tilt can go.
2. **The cougar's stalking crouch sinks its paws.** The drawing lowers a crouching cat's whole body by about a third of its leg, so its paws go into the ground (0.06). The check now treats the crouch as a declared pose. In the film the cougar lies in the ferns, and they hide its paws. *Recommendation:* leave it for the film. Later, have the drawing fold the legs instead of sinking the body (it shows in play too, whenever a cat stalks on open ground).
3. **Changes to the approved shots** (table above): the stalk camera moved 3 m, a slightly wider lens on her head-up, the fence take-off 1 m earlier, and a shorter map glide. *Recommendation:* accept them. Each is the smallest change that passes, and each keeps the shot's intent. Your rough-cut notes will tell if any reads wrong.
4. **Add the motion check to the browser suite** (f1.md question 4, now that it passes)? It takes about 15 s for all 12 layers. *Recommendation:* yes, in F4, once the final render is in. A game change that breaks the film (moved trees, a drawing change) would then show up before an itch.io upload. It would need renaming to `*-browser-check.mjs`.
5. **The sound is still timed for 40 s** (`SOUND` in `intro-film-shots.mjs`). *Recommendation:* F4 retimes it to the 33 s beats (`BEATS`: head up 16.0, bolt 17.0, pounce 17.93, fence 21.64) and adds the hush in the slow motion.

## New ideas noticed

- **The motion check's speed limits never applied by species** before this fix (the probe gave a number, not a name). Other tools that copied that probe pattern might do the same. A one-line assert (that every judged animal's species is in the limits table) would catch it.
- **The drawing turns an animal to face its movement almost at once** (about 0.3 rad a frame when the doe set off). Real deer whirl fast, but it may look snappy in play for slow animals that start walking off at an angle. The motion sweep's heading-snap measure could check this at the start of a walk.
- **The plan's paths could drive the website's staged clips** (`tools/capture-site-video.mjs`): the same eased curves and cameras, and the same check before anything is filmed.
- **A per-shot "safe spot" search** (as used for the stalk camera: nearest camera spot whose lines to the real drawn heads and bodies stay clear) could be a flag on `intro-film-sightlines.mjs`. Then a moved tree in the game suggests the fix as well as reporting the failure.
