# Session 7/10: Smooth first 10 s, faster loading on weak laptops

## Done

- **New probe, `tools/first-seconds-profile.mjs`.** It loads a first visit (no saved farm) and times each loading step. Then it records every frame for 30 s after the loading screen goes, in windows (0–5, 5–10, 10–20, 20–30 s). It also lists the slowest frames with where they went, and the long tasks outside frames. `FS_PROFILE=1` adds the top functions and who called the slow WebGL waits. Listed in the perf skill and in decision 0038.
- **The main cause of the choppy opening: field-guide portraits.** From 1.5 s after the UI was built, `warmPortraits` drew every species' portrait in the background, one every 400 ms at most. Each one meant:
  - a full shader compile in a second WebGL context, because the material was thrown away after each portrait, so the program was too;
  - a read back from the graphics chip (`trim`'s `getImageData`).

  On the slow stand-in this was 7.7 s of the first 10 s of main-thread time, in about 1 s blocks. That fits the player numbers: 12–25 fps at the 10 s sample, rising to 35–60 by 30 s, once the queue was done.
  - Now the portraits wait until 30 s after loading ends and run only in idle time (`PORTRAIT_WARM_DELAY_MS`, timeout 3 s).
  - `renderPortrait` keeps one material per shader key, so a program is compiled once per look, not once per species. A portrait asked for sooner (the guide, an animal's panel, watch mode) is still drawn on the spot, and cheaper than before.
- **`renderer.gpuName()` is now asked right after the renderer is made.** Its first call used to come straight after the first draw, inside the `game_loaded` event. There it waited for the chip to finish that frame: 1.1 s on the stand-in, in the last loading step.
- **Loading no longer stops when nothing is painted.** `nextPaint` (main.js) waited for an animation frame. A background tab, or an itch.io frame scrolled out of view, paints none, so loading stalled until the player looked again. Now each step continues after 250 ms without a paint. Why: in PostHog, several slow loads in the last 14 days sat 15–20 s or more between 'Opening your saved farms' and 'Preparing the land'. That step is only the save store and one `nextPaint`. Not measured headless. The PostHog step times will show whether those gaps go away.
- **What the slow loads are made of** (PostHog `game_loaded.steps`, loads over 15 s in the last 14 days, mostly Chromebooks and older Macs on Fast):
  - Page start to 'Opening your saved farms', i.e. downloading and starting the game's 239 JS files (5.8 MB, unminified): 3–10 s.
  - 'Welcoming the wild' to 'Catching the light', i.e. building the first frame's land, plants and animals: 4–8 s.
  - 'Catching the light' to 'Ready', i.e. shader warm-up plus the first draw: 4–11 s.
  - The rest is under 2 s.
- **Checked and ruled out:**
  - Tree impostor baking (decision 0042): 10 crown models and 20 ms on PNW.
  - The import chain: 6 levels deep, so about 1–2 s of round trips on a slow link.
  - The loading screen's scene: CSS-animated SVG.

## Open questions for Cole

1. **Should the 10 s and 30 s frame samples count from the end of loading?** Today they count from partway through loading, so a slow load's "10 s" sample can land before the first frame, and a fast load's covers only the first few seconds of play. Recommend yes, but as its own small change after this batch's numbers are read, so the before/after compares like with like. A new `from_ready: true` property would keep old and new apart.
2. **Minify the itch.io and website build?** The game is 239 files, 5.8 MB, sent as written. The slow loads spend 3–10 s before the first loading step. A minify step in the build (`tools/build-itch.sh` and the Pages workflow; the source stays unbundled) would cut it to roughly 2 MB, less if itch's CDN compresses. Recommend: first check whether itch serves the files gzip-compressed (one look at the live page's network panel). If it doesn't, add a minify step, which is a build-pipeline change with its own session.
3. **Portraits after 30 s.** On a weak laptop, the field guide opened in the first 30 s now draws its portraits on the spot: about one shader compile per look (a few per map), not per species. Recommend: keep it this way. Opening the guide that early is rare, and the opening is what players judge.

## New ideas noticed

- **Fix `animal-density-check` before this branch merges.** It already fails at HEAD e0ed367, with a lone rabbit's "Where animals are" density of 0.18 against the 0.2 limit. CI's headless check will block the PR. It probably comes from session 5's starting animals or session 6's edge forest.

- **The first draw on a weak chip still waits on shaders.** On SwiftShader that is 2.7 s in the first draw, mostly a USE_INSTANCING + USE_MAP + USE_SHADOWMAP program and the sun's 3 shadow programs (three.js keeps their materials private). PostHog's 'Catching the light' to 'Ready' is 4–11 s on Chromebooks. Two options:
  - warm the shadow programs with a hidden one-pixel shadow render during 'Catching the light';
  - start that step's compile while the plants are being built.

  Both need care, because three.js's shadow materials are internal.
- **'Welcoming the wild' to 'Catching the light' is 4–8 s on Chromebooks**, against 0.3 s here. A CPU profile of `prepareFirstFrame` at CPU ×10 with the hardware GPU would show whether it's the terrain build (`buildWater`, `updateSurface`), the plants or the animals. The next target after shaders.
- **`warmAnimals` and `warmRemains` still run in the first seconds.** At CPU ×10 that is 60–150 ms blocks (`bakeSurface`, `simplifyAnimal`), about 1–1.5 s of the first 5 s. They prevent first-appearance stalls (decision 0038), so they stay. They could start a few seconds later on Fast.
- **`trim` in `thumbnails.js` reads pixels back** for every tool icon and portrait. Trimming to the model's projected bounds instead would skip the read back.
