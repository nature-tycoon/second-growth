# Film rework F2/4: shot list and top-down map

## Done

- **The shot list**, [film-shots.md](film-shots.md): 12 shots (counting the opening black), about 33 s, in plain film directions: time, what we see, camera (still / slow push / pan / rise, and the lens), where it stands, what the animals do, sound. It follows your story: doe in the sunlit gap, her ears, the cougar's eyes in the ferns (and visible, tiny, in the first shot), slow stalk, head snaps up, real-speed burst with 1.5 s of half-speed slow motion at the missed pounce, the fence leap by the waterfall, the cougar pulls up and watches, the rise, 30 years on, the rewind to the start view.
- **Where it happens.** Everything is at the spot where the creek leaves the old forest north of the farm (tile 69). Above the fence, the border forest draws the creek's line as a strip of salmonberry with alders on both sides (bare in March) and no conifers. That's a natural sunlit gap, 12 m wide, with clear sight lines along it and across it. The doe browses 40 m north of the fence. The cougar lies 13 m west of her and creeps to 9 m. The chase runs 35 m to the last trees, and the doe clears the fence 10 m east of the creek, then runs down the far bank past the falls. Every animal stays within 6 tiles of the farm (the limit is 9, because of the ground bug).
- **One source for the plan**: `tools/intro-film-plan.mjs` holds the shots (cameras, lenses, times, words), the animals' paths with time marks (real seconds) and the slow motion. The written list, the map and the check all read it. F3 can stage straight from it, so the film can't drift from what you approved.
- **The check**: `nice -n 15 node tools/intro-film-sightlines.mjs` (about 5 s). It loads the start game in headless Chrome, with no images and nothing drawn. For every shot, every 0.1 s, it casts lines from the camera to each animal's head and body. It uses F1's blocker against the real trunks (as the forest draws them), logs, bushes and the drawn ground. It also checks that the animal is in the frame at the shot's lens, the camera isn't in a trunk, the camera's speed and pan rate, the animals' real speeds, the cougar never getting ahead of the doe, and the 9-tile limit. It writes the land and the results to `tools/intro-film-map.json` (119 kB, committed, so the map page works without running anything). Not named `*-check.mjs`: it needs a GPU browser. The only code change outside the new files: `intro-film-probe.mjs` now exports `meshGround`, `obstacles` and `blocker`.
- **The map**: `http://localhost:8347/tools/film-shots.html`, linked from the review page ("Shot list and map →"). It's drawn from the real map data: terrain with height bands, the creek and ponds, every trunk, shrubs, the fence, the waterfall, the farm edge and the 9-tile line. Each camera is a numbered dot with its view cone, and each animal's path has film-time marks (hover a time for what happens). A "Whole farm" view shows the rise, the glide and the start view. Click a shot row to light its camera. Drag the time bar to see the animals move and the current camera's cone. I measured it in the browser pane at 1440×900 and 1280×800 (two-column layout, no console errors) and checked it with three small screenshots. Tab closed after.

### What the check can't see (for F3)

- **Ferns, low conifer branches and leaves don't block** sight lines, and bushes are balls. The cougar "low in the sword ferns" depends on how tall the drawn ferns are. If they're too tall, its eyes are hidden. If too short, it isn't "in" them. Only the rough cut shows this.
- Head and body heights are estimates from the first cut's probe (doe 0.22 browsing to 0.52 alert, crouched cougar 0.24). F3's motion check measures the real pose.
- The restored farm's shots (10, 11) are map views from high up and aren't sight-line tested. That farm's trees differ from the bare farm's.
- Animals move in straight lines between marks at constant speed. F3 will smooth the corners and the starts and stops.

## Open questions for Cole

1. **Length: 33 s, not 30.** The stalk and the watch need time to read. *Recommendation:* approve 33 s. If you want 30, trim the stalk (shot 4) by 1 s and the rise (shot 9) by 1 s, and start the dissolve 1 s earlier.
2. **The creek doesn't run inside the forest.** The game draws the creek only from the fence south. Above it, the forest shows a salmonberry strip with alders, not water, so "where the creek leaves the trees" has no water in the forest shots. *Recommendation:* film it as is (the water comes in at the falls, shot 7). Drawing the creek through the border forest is a separate game change; see new ideas.
3. **The cougar in shot 1 is tiny** (38 m away on a wide lens, a few pixels at 720p). That fits "there all along" on a second viewing, but nobody notices it the first time. *Recommendation:* keep it tiny. Shot 3 is the reveal, and spotting it on a rewatch is the reward. If you want it findable, F3 can use a slightly longer lens in shot 1 (40° instead of 46°).
4. **"Misty"**: the film runs with weather off. The early-morning haze of the renderer's sky is the mist. *Recommendation:* keep the early-morning look as the first cut had it (time of day 0.07). A real fog effect would be new drawing work.
5. **The rise crosses two farms.** It starts on today's farm (the cougar still at the edge) and dissolves to the restored farm mid-rise, so F3 must film the same camera move on both. *Recommendation:* yes, as planned. It's what makes the dissolve read as "same place, 30 years on".
6. **Slow motion sound**: I wrote "drops to a low hush" for the 1.5 s of slow motion. *Recommendation:* a hush (sound slowed or low-passed), not silence. Silence reads as a glitch.
