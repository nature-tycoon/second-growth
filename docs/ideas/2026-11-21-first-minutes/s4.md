# Session 4/10: Watch it grow, follow a deer

## Done
- **"Watch 10 years"** (`js/ui/timelapse.js`). Five seconds after a first visit's first planting, once the opening is over, a note offers it: "Your seeds are in. See what ten years make of them?" with one button. It's offered once per farm (`flags.lapseOffered`). The Menu also has "Watch 10 years (a time-lapse)" for anyone at any time, with no prompt. Returning players never get the prompt.
  - It runs the farm at the top speed (8 game days a second, about 2.5 minutes for ten years) inside watch mode with no controls on screen. Watch's director (`Watch.lapseShot`) drifts slowly over a random patch of what the player planted, at a zoom close enough to see it grow, and every third shot follows an animal within 10 tiles of the planting if one is out. With nothing planted (from the Menu), it drifts over whatever trees and shrubs there are. The top letterbox bar shows "Year 3 of 10 · click to stop" and the date.
  - It ends on its own after ten game years, or after 3.5 real minutes on a machine too slow to get there. Any click, tap, key or wheel also ends it, and that input does nothing else: no paint, no pause. Then one line, for example "Ten years on: 140 trees and 60 shrubs stand where you planted, and Roosevelt elk and black bear have moved in. Health 18 → 34." (counts on the planted tiles, new species on the farm, health before and after).
  - It stops early, pauses and says why when money drops under $1,000 (and below where it started), or when fire reaches a planted tile (it flies there). Other fires and floods play out: no banner, and no "pause on events" pause. Keystone moments wait until it's over (`isBusy`), so they don't drop the speed to normal mid-way. The Story offer waits while the time-lapse offer is up or running.
  - The simulation is unchanged: it's the usual top speed.
- **Follow a deer** (`js/ui/opening.js`, `Watch.follow`). Once the first deer is 4 tiles onto the field after the fence comes out, a note reads "They're in." with one button: "Follow a black-tailed deer". It starts watch mode at eye level, locked onto that deer (Done or Esc comes back, and the eye setting goes back to what it was). The culvert prompt waits while the offer is up (12 s) or the deer is being followed, then comes as before. It's offered once per first visit (`flags.opening.followOffered`). Maps without a fence step don't get it.

## Open questions for Cole
1. **Should the time-lapse also be on the speed controls, not only in the Menu?** My recommendation: Menu only for now. The speed bar is already tight on phones in landscape. If `timelapse_started { from: menu }` shows people look for it, add a small button there.
2. **After the time-lapse the game goes back to normal speed, not top speed.** My recommendation: keep it that way. The player has just been away from the controls for 2.5 minutes, and normal speed is the gentler place to pick up again.
3. **Follow-a-deer delays the culvert prompt by up to 12 s if it's ignored.** My recommendation: fine. It's one short beat, and it's the moment the deer are most worth looking at.
4. **The time-lapse can't be sped past the top speed, so a slow laptop (under ~25 fps) sees fewer than ten years before the 3.5-minute cap ends it.** My recommendation: keep the cap. The summary says how many years it really was ("Seven years on: …").
5. **The cougar is about 8 game years away for a player who restores as thoroughly as the scripted plan, likely more than 15 for a typical first visit, and 20+ untended.** My recommendation: the long goal shows a nearer step first, in session 10 (minutes 5–15). The steps: deer on the field (minute one, already done), then an owl hunting the meadow (year ~3), then elk coming down to the creek (year ~6), then the cougar. Each is a real rule already in the sim. One ten-year time-lapse can reach the owl and the elk for a player who keeps planting.
6. **For a single meadow stroke, the summary line is thin** ("what you sowed is grown in" plus health, and any newcomers). My recommendation: fine for now. The picture is the payoff. The before/after card under New ideas would carry it better than more words.

## New ideas noticed
- The summary line could become a small before/after card: a picture of the planted patch on the first day and on the last.
- The time-lapse could hold a beat on a new species the first time one is seen (the "has arrived" moment), since keystone moments wait until it's over.
- A sound bed for the time-lapse: the seasons' ambience changing fast (birdsong, rain, snow quiet) instead of the usual mix.
- The follow offer could come back later for the first elk or the first bear, once per species, outside the first visit.
