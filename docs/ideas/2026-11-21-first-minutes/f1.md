# Film rework F1/4: film checks and review page

## Done

- **Motion check**, `tools/intro-film-motion.mjs`. It runs the film's own staging frame by frame in headless Chrome. The game's drawing poses the animals, but nothing is drawn on the GPU and no images are made. All six layers take about 7 s. It prints one table per shot and exits non-zero on any failure. The limits are in one table at the top of the file, each with a one-line reason. `--cached` judges the last probe again, `--layers=chase` probes only some layers, and `--detail` prints when each failure happens. The raw numbers go to `dist/intro-film/motion-probe.json` and the verdicts to `motion-report.json`.
  - The reading side is `tools/intro-film-probe.mjs`, which runs in the studio page (`intro-film.html?layer=X&probe=1`). Each frame it reads the camera (position, look direction, look point, field of view, roll) and each cast animal as drawn: hoof level against the **drawn terrain mesh** under four hoof spots, the head (where the model has its eyes), the body, the body length, the leg cycle (`legPhase`) and its place in the frame. It also casts sight lines from the camera to the head and body. They are tested against trunks and snags (from what flora drew, which also covers the border forest), logs (`logSpot`), the terrain mesh, and bushes (approximated as balls).
  - `tools/intro-film.mjs`: frames now go through `advance(f)`, which stages, sets the camera and draws in order. The filmed frames are unchanged. `probe(f)` is the same thing with the GPU draw stubbed out.
  - Declared in the script (`tools/intro-film-shots.mjs`): `SUBJECTS` (each shot's animals, the key one first), `PREDATOR` (the cougar, its prey, and when it attacks) and `SLOWMO` (`[from, to, rate]`, which scales measured speeds back up; empty today). Jumps come from the staging (`a.hunt.leap`).
  - It is **not** named `*-check.mjs`. `check-release.mjs` runs every `tools/*-check.mjs` in the headless PR suite, and this check needs a GPU browser and fails on today's film by design, so with that name it would block every PR.
- **Rough cuts**: `nice -n 15 node tools/film-intro.mjs --rough` films every layer at 640×360, 10 fps (every third frame of the 30 fps clock, the skipped frames posed but not drawn), with no sound. It writes `dist/intro-film/rough/intro-film-rough.mp4` (2.9 MB) in about 15 s on the M5. It touches nothing in `assets/` or `website/`. I asked for about 12 fps and used 10: 30 fps divides evenly by 3, and every layer starts on a multiple of 3.
- **Contact sheets**: assembly makes one (a 160-px frame for each second, in one row). A full render writes the game copy's to `dist/intro-film/contact/intro-film.jpg`, and a rough cut writes `intro-film-rough.jpg`. `--contact=<video>` makes one from an existing cut; today's film got one this way. The sheets live in `dist/`, not `assets/`, because itch ships all of `assets/` and a sheet would add 138 kB to the game download.
- **Review page**: `http://localhost:8347/tools/film-review.html` (`?cut=dist/intro-film/rough/intro-film-rough.mp4` for a rough cut; the default is `assets/intro/intro-film.mp4`). It has:
  - the player;
  - a shot bar with the script's shot names (click to jump) and marks for notes;
  - the contact strip (click a frame to jump; the current second is highlighted). If a cut has no sheet, the page makes one itself from the video;
  - a "Note at 0:12.4" button (or N) that pauses and adds a note at that time, labelled with its shot. Notes are kept per cut in that browser;
  - "Copy notes", which copies them as plain text (`0:16.0 (chase): cougar ahead of the deer`), ready to paste into the chat. Nothing is written to the server.

  I checked it once in the browser pane by measuring it, plus one quarter-size screenshot. On the rough cut it played 40 s at 640×360 with 40 thumbnails and six shots. Seeking from a thumbnail, adding notes and copying all worked. It made its own sheet for the 1080p cut. `intro-film-check.mjs` still passes.

**Causes found along the way** (for F3):
- **The sinking.** Past 9 tiles outside the map, the game's drawing takes the ground's height at row −9: `ground = w.heightAt(clamp(a.x, -9, …), clamp(a.y, -9, …))` in `js/render3d/actors-common.js:134`. The land itself is drawn 14 tiles out (`BORDER`). The chase line runs to row −9.8 and the cougar's cover is at −11, so both are drawn inside the hillside. Outside the map, the same function also takes the terrain as river (`t = inside ? … : T.RIVER`, line 131), which may set off a water pose. I haven't checked that.
- **Speed.** The staging runs the deer at 3.25 tiles/s and the cougar at 2.65. The legs keep the game's own stride for the ground covered, so too fast a run also gives too fast a leg cycle. By its drawn body lengths the deer's gallop looks almost real (11.6 m/s-equivalent). Its speed over the ground (26 m/s past trunks 8 m apart) and its legs (6 Hz) are what look frantic.
- **The cougar ahead.** Its cover (51.7, −10.7) is 3.4 tiles further along the chase than the deer's browsing spot (48.3, −8.6).

What the checks can't see (uncertain):
- Tree crowns, low conifer branches and ferns don't block sight lines. Bushes are balls. So "subject in sight" can pass while leaves hide a head.
- Hooves are four points at ±0.32 of the body length, not the model's real hooves. A gait pose that dips the body isn't measured.
- "Across the frame" is how fast the animal crosses the frame for a still camera at the shot's lens, not the on-screen motion of a camera that tracks it.
- Camera speeds use the 8 m tile, but the scene isn't to scale vertically: eye height is 0.46 tiles, or 3.7 m.

## Open questions for Cole

1. **What counts as "real speed"?** The check holds the animals to the map's scale (8 m a tile), to their own drawn body lengths and to their leg cycle. Today the deer fails on the map's scale and on its legs, but passes on body lengths. *Recommendation:* keep all three. Running the deer at the map's scale (at most 1.6 tiles/s) also halves its leg rate, and that is what looked frantic.
2. **The restored farm's elk walk at 8.6–11 m/s** in the rise shot (the game's normal pace, decision 0021's two clocks). The check fails them, though you didn't flag them. *Recommendation:* in the film, run the restored farm's animals at watch pace or slower, so they walk about 1.5 m/s. In play they stay as they are.
3. **The map-view glide** at the end moves 0.55 screen widths a second (limit 0.35). *Recommendation:* slow it in F3. The ~30 s film shortens that part anyway.
4. **Should the motion check run before each itch.io upload** once the re-staged film passes (as a browser check)? It takes about 10 s. *Recommendation:* yes, so a later game change that breaks the film (trees moved, a drawing change) shows up before release.
5. **Fix the ground-height bug in the game?** Animals more than 9 tiles outside the farm are drawn sunk into the border land (up to 0.78 here). *Recommendation:* yes, as a one-line fix in its own small change (clamp to `BORDER`, then the motion sweep). F3 can also simply keep the chase within 9 tiles of the edge.
