# Session 7: Plants look natural

## Done

- **Trees in creeks die** (`drownOdds`, `inChannel` in `js/sim/plants.js`; rules in 0028).
  - What was actually wrong: no tree ever grows on a creek *tile*. The trees standing "in" creeks
    grew on bank tiles, where the creek's drawn water reaches up the bank and over their trunks. There
    were 1–2 of those per map. The bigger gaps were in held water:
    - A wet-tolerant tree (Oregon ash, red maple, river birch, sycamore) lived in a beaver pond forever.
    - Every other flooded tree died on exactly its 30th wet day, the whole stand at once.
    - A tree in water shallower than about 35 cm never died.
  - Now a tree whose drawn trunk stands in a flowing creek or river dies within a season (8% a day),
    tolerant or not, and leaves a snag or log through the normal `removeTree` path. No tree seeds
    where its trunk would stand in the creek.
  - **New beaver ponds:** dry-ground trees and bushes start drowning after 20 wet days, at odds of
    1 in 25 a day. Most of a drowned stand dies over the next one or two seasons, standing as snags,
    like a real beaver meadow. This also applies in shallow water.
  - **Wet-tolerant trees** (`wetOK`, a trait already in the data) live on the soggy margin
    indefinitely. In water over about 35 cm deep they survive two years (`floodDays`, 240 days,
    overridable per species), then die over the following months.
  - Mangroves, swamp trees and water plants never drown.
- **Leaf-out by the day** (`plantPhaseOn` in `js/data/plants.js`, new record 0051). This is drawing
  only; food and shade still go by the month.
  - Every plant has its own leaf-out day. Order: bushes first, then grass and herbs, then trees, then
    oaks and Oregon ash (new `leafOut: 6` field, about two weeks later).
  - Individual plants of one species vary by up to 5 days, and the highest ground is up to 3 days
    later.
  - On Hollis, 10% to 90% of deciduous plants leaf out over about 12 game days (four calendar weeks).
    Oaks follow into mid-April.
  - Autumn colour and leaf fall spread out the same way: the first trees colour in early October and
    the last drop in early December. Coolest ground goes first.
  - Blossom and fruit shift a few days either side of their month, so a whole orchard doesn't flower
    on the 1st.
  - Chinandega's dry forest leafs out the same gradual way when the rains come.
  - The plant drawing redraws only tiles whose phase changed, every 2 game days, at about 1–2 ms per
    game day on a 120×90 map.
- **Patchy shrubs and grass**
  - Sim (rule in 0027): when birds carry bush seed (berries, with fruit-eaters on the map), 60% of it
    is lost unless it lands within a tile of a perch (a grown tree, a snag or a fence). Berry bushes
    now gather at wood edges, round lone trees and along fence lines, and spread out from there,
    instead of speckling the whole meadow. This is the real nucleation pattern.
  - In 10 years on Hollis (restore plan), bush cover fell from 34% to 22% of land, and clumpiness (block
    variance over mean) rose from 4.7 to 5.0. Atlanta: 46% → 36%, clumpiness 3.2 → 4.5. Chinandega:
    19% → 12%, 6.1 → 6.5.
  - Drawing (`flora.js`), with no effect on the sim:
    - Grass is drawn thick in clumps a couple of tiles across and thin between them. A tile now gets
      0.3× to 2× the usual tufts, averaging the same, so the instance count holds.
    - The tufts of a clump gather round a centre shared by a 2-tile cell instead of one centre per
      tile, so clumps cross tile edges.
    - Bushes spread over most of their tile instead of the middle 40%, so thickets lose their grid rows.
    - Bush sizes vary in patches a few tiles across (0.72–1.28×).
    - A bush with bushes on all four sides is drawn 22% bigger, so a thicket's heart closes into one
      mass.

## Open questions for Cole

1. **Should red alder and black cottonwood count as wet-tolerant?** Today only Oregon ash is, on
   Hollis. Both grow on wet bottomland in real life, but both die in a permanent beaver pond within a
   few years. *Recommend: leave them as they are.* As dry-ground trees they drown in a pond within a
   season or two. They still grow right up to the creek, because the bank isn't standing water.
2. **Leaf-out redraws more plant regions in spring and autumn.** In the perf-guard fixture, regions
   redrawn per 100 frames rose 28–42%, because mature woods that daily growth wouldn't touch now redraw
   as their trees leaf out. play-profile shows no frame-time change at play and top speed. *Recommend: keep it and accept the new perf-guard baseline.*
3. **Should leaf-out also change shade and food?** Today it's drawing only. Shade and fruit still flip
   by the month, so a bush can be drawn bare for a few days while the sim already shades with its
   leaves. *Recommend: leave the sim on the month.* Moving it would shift every forest-floor plant's
   light and the balance, for a gap of a few days.
4. **Should the ground's grass tint (the terrain under the tufts) green up by the day too?** It still
   greens on the 1st. *Recommend: leave it as it is.* The tufts on top carry the change, and per-tile
   terrain repaints cost more than plants.
