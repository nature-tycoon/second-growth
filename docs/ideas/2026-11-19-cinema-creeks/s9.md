- **The crawl had four causes. None was the 0.65 squeeze pace, which is why tuning it only moved the crawl around.**
  1. **A buffalo stuck on a marsh tile** (the biggest). `settle` walked it toward a spot it had kept from an earlier walk. A grazer had taken that spot since, so `walkStep` stopped short at once, and the buffalo flipped between walk and idle every two frames for seconds without moving. Now it walks to a dry spot that is free, chosen fresh (`steering.js`).
  2. **Followers pressing head-on into a standing herd mate.** They edged forward at 0.65 pace and `separate` shoved them back. They crept just fast enough to keep resetting the stall timer. Now a follower that touches one standing right in front of it stops there and drops its slot (`spacing.standingAhead`, on the ground only). Any other walker goes round after about a second (`a.pressT`, not saved).
  3. **Nose to nose with no way round.** A hippo and a crocodile met in a river channel, and an elk queue piled up behind its stuck leader. They pressed until the 3-day stall timer ran out (about 4 s). Now a walker touching others that makes no progress for a day re-routes. This applies to walks only, and not to a herd swimming its crossing.
  4. **A deer at a building's corner.** Its line to the next point clipped a blocked tile corner. It already faced that point, so its pivot turned nothing and its step stayed at zero. Now it slides along the wall by whichever x or y part of the step is clear, turning toward the slide (`smoothWalk`).
- **The regression review found five problems, and all five were fixed before commit.**
  - A stopped follower kept pressing the same slot.
  - Re-routing fired in packed river crossings and on drinkers' approaches to the bank.
  - The wall slide moved bodies sideways.
  - `settle` skipped a thirsty animal's bank spot.
  - The 0025 rule needed updating.

## Open questions for Cole

1. **Is the crawl limit sound on two seeds?** No. On the same code, one seed pair fails and the next passes. Hippo crawl swung between 0.3 and 1.4 per 1000 across my variants, none of which touched water animals; only the random stream changed. *Recommend: keep the limit (4 per 1000), but judge movement changes by the 20-seed probe: no species over the limit on all seeds together, and no more failing pairs than before. The two-seed sweep stays the quick CI gate.*
2. **Hippo crawl is 0.27 → 0.51 per 1000, the one number above HEAD.** These are short tails of 20–40 frames, each just past the 2-second hold, and the level is within the noise above. *Recommend: accept it as noise. Don't tune for it.*
3. **The two crawl pairs left are PNW family leads (deer on 7,42, which is unchanged from HEAD, and elk on 303,404, which fell from 17.3 to 6.3).** Each lead slows to 0.5–0.7 pace on purpose (`rangePaces`) while its family lags. Edging slows it further, and the product falls under the crawl line. *Recommend: a small follow-up where a lead waiting for its family stops and looks back instead of creeping. The sweep's pace already allows for trotting, wading and `a.pace`, but not for `rangePaces`. Don't change the measure.*
4. **Serengeti herbivore biomass is far over its reference (696 at HEAD, 750 now, against 100–200).** It's not caused by this session, but it's large. *Recommend: a `/balance` session for Serengeti grazers.*

## New ideas noticed

- The `wet` count in the sweep never saw the stuck buffalo. It counts only animals standing still, and the buffalo was in `walk` at the end of each frame. Counting a land walker that stays on a water tile across many frames, whatever its state, would catch it.
- Chinandega coati in a trunk (114–122 per 1000 on seeds 11,13) is unchanged from HEAD and fails even with all seeds together. It's worth its own look.
- On some single pairs, buffalo and hippo `clump` sits just over the limit (4.7–5.5), much as at HEAD. A herd room that spreads a resting pod in water as well as on the ground would be more consistent, but it would change river crossings, so it needs care.
- `tools/herd-crawl-probe.mjs` takes about 10 minutes for all maps. A `--counts=crawl,clump` filter and a JSON diff against a saved baseline would make before/after comparisons quicker.
