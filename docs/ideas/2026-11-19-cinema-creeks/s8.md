# Session 8: Serengeti herd routes

## Done

- **The migration follows green grass within its seasonal loop** (`js/sim/migration-route.js`, new record 0052). It applies to every species with `crossing` (wildebeest, zebra).
  - On this range the loop shows as the June–October passage: the herds swim the river in the south and work north to the park. Nothing about when they come or go changed.
  - **Where they cross** now changes from year to year. They swim over where the grass beyond the far bank is greenest, anywhere in the middle 40% of the river. Before, they crossed at the same spot every year. Groups that arrive while a crossing is under way join it, so the crocodiles and the crossing moment work as before.
  - **Their route** follows green grass: fresh, uneaten and on moist ground, and greenest a month after a fire. Leaders pick the greener patches on their walks and drift north as the season goes on, lingering where the grass is good. The rest of the herd stands on green grass near their places instead of on bare ground or in the bush. A herd that has eaten or dried out its patch moves on sooner.
  - Nothing new is saved. Population, carrying capacity, season months and the fence block are unchanged.
- **Measured** with the new `tools/migration-route-probe.mjs`: a restored range with the fences down, 11 seeds × 8 years, before vs after.
  - The routes differ from year to year: the same month's herd position is 19.8 tiles apart between years, against 5.3 before.
  - They still go south to north: 97% of moves from one month to the next go north or hold (98% before).
  - They are on greener grass, but only a little: standing and feeding, 0.82× the range's average green against 0.76× before.
  - Why the gain is small: a restored range is evenly lush, so there is little greener ground to find. The herds also eat down whatever they stand on. The pasture they choose, leaving aside their own grazing, is only slightly greener (1.09× vs 1.06×). Stronger settings didn't help.

## Open questions for Cole

1. **Should the herds sweep further north before they leave?** They drift north but usually reach only the middle of the range (rows 65–78 of 90) by October, then walk off north. Most of their time goes to water trips to the river and creeks. Real herds also stay near water in the dry season.
   *Recommended:* leave it. It looks like grazing herds working slowly north, and pulling them harder away from water would be less natural.
2. **Should arrival timing vary with the year's rain?** Right now the herds come and go by fixed season months. Only their route and crossing follow the grass. Real herds come early in a dry year and late in a wet one, but changing that would touch population, goals ("20 wildebeest at once") and the wildlife panel's season text.
   *Recommended:* not now. The route and crossing variation is what shows on screen.
3. **Should resident grazers follow green grass too?** Gazelle, topi, hartebeest and buffalo could use the same greenness rule on their walks. It would be a small change, but it would touch balance on every grazing map.
   *Recommended:* yes, later, as its own session with seeded calibrate runs on Serengeti and Chinandega.

## New ideas noticed

- Migrant leaders make about one decision every three days, and most go to water trips. Long "roam" trips almost never fire for them (13 tries, 4 taken, in 5 seasons). Worth a look if herds ever seem to dawdle.
- A post-fire green flush is now something herds seek out. A controlled burn early in the dry season could become a player tool for drawing the migration somewhere ("burn to bring the herds"), which is real Serengeti practice. It could get a line in the fire tool's text and the field guide.
- The probe could also run on a part-restored range with burns, where green grass is patchy and the route choice should show much more than on an evenly lush range.
