## Open questions for Cole

1. **Downstream flow.** The creek below a dam keeps running at its usual level, so I didn't make it "drop".
   - The game doesn't track how much water a creek carries, only its level.
   - Real beaver dams leak and keep the creek below flowing through summer, often more steadily than before.
   - Lowering the creek below a dam would also strand fish and break their routes.

   *Recommend: leave it as is. If you want something you can see, session 6 (waterfalls) could draw a thin overflow trickle down the face of the dam when the pond is full.*
2. **Dam sometimes not drawn until a later redraw.**
   - In one of its two browser runs, the visual check saw the new dam's mounds appear only after it forced the plants to redraw. That was 7 days after the dam was built.
   - In the other run, the dam appeared after 2 days.
   - The code that decides when to redraw already tracks the dam tile, and I didn't change it. So the cause may be how the check advanced time rather than the game. Not confirmed.

   *Recommend: a short follow-up check. After a dam is built and one day has passed in the normal game loop, assert that the dam is drawn.*
3. **Trees on the tiles a dam reaches across.** Only the dam's own tile stops plants growing, so a tree on the bank where a dam end lands could stand through the mound.

   *Recommend: when the dam is built, cut down the woody plants where its ends land. Beavers fell those trees for the dam anyway.*
4. **Two dams close together.** A pond next to another dam's marsh edge can come out slightly bigger than the site test predicted. This can happen when ponds lie within about 4 tiles of each other. Building it no closer than 9 tiles keeps that rare.

   *Recommend: leave it.*
