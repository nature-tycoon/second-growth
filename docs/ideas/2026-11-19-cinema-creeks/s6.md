# Session 6: Waterfalls

## Done

- **A natural waterfall on Hollis Farm (PNW)** at (69,6), in the ditch's steep reach in the foothills
  below its spring (`creekFalls`, `js/maps/landform.js`). The creek runs off a lip and drops 2.1 levels
  (about 5 m) in one tile into a plunge pool at (69,7). A short gorge below eases back to the old bed
  within 3 tiles, and rocks stand either side of the fall.
  - How it's made: the creek already fell 1.4 m every 8 m there. The pass gathers that drop at one
    ledge, the way Cascade creeks drop over a basalt ledge. It only ever lowers the land, and only on a
    straight reach one tile wide.
- **Which maps get falls** (logged per map in `waterfall-check`): **PNW only.** Maps opt in, and only
  PNW does. The other maps' creeks have no steep reach for a fall to form on, so none would get one
  anyway:
  - Atlanta: at most 0.36 levels a tile
  - Chinandega: at most 0.39 a tile
  - Serengeti: at most 0.25 a tile
  - Amazon: a single 0.7 step at the bluff
  - Sumatra: a single 1.08 step into the peat
- **Pool holds its level, never floods.** No new water solver and no new saved state.
  - The pool's own shape holds it. It stands 0.2 levels (about 50 cm) over its tail-out, which is its
    lowest way out, and far under its side banks. The creek below always stands lower, so water steps
    down and flows on. This uses the same "fill to the lowest way out" rule as session 5, but built
    into the pool's shape rather than as a new basin.
  - The check holds the pool's level through a year of weather, and through a save and load.
  - Raise, Lower and Fill & grade beside the fall or the pool change nothing (creek corners belong to
    the creek, 0048). A pond dug beside the pool joins it, and the pool drops 0.08 (20 cm).
- **Fish:** no river fish can swim up the fall, by any route. The fall's upper part stands far above
  its own water, so the existing depth test refuses it. No species leaps, and none was added.
- **Beavers:** a dam below the fall never builds high enough to drown it (`damCrest` passes over that
  height and picks a lower one).
- **Drawing:**
  - The water runs white over the lip and foams out across the pool (`aWhite` in the water shader).
  - A curved curtain of water hangs from the lip to the pool (`buildFalls`): one mesh, 16 triangles,
    one draw call, PNW only.
- **Bug fixed on every map:** the bank beside a steep creek took the water level from a tile upstream.
  So beside the PNW ditch's steep reach, the drawn water stood up to 0.6 levels (1.4 m) over the
  creek's own, above its banks. It now takes the water beside it first. `pond-edge-measure` shows no
  change on any map.
- **Seen at eye level** in the game: a white sheet over the lip into a pool, with the creek running on
  below.
- **Checks:**
  - New `tools/waterfall-check.mjs`, 8 checks.
  - water, hollis-water, terrain-relief, terrain-edit (45), fish-water, beaver-pond, beaver-dam,
    dug-creek, tool-actions, species-name-count, perf-guard (all within 10%): all pass.
  - Motion sweep pnw: within limits both before and after. Seeded per-species numbers moved a little
    because the land changed, for example otter stalls 1 → 7 per 1000 frames against a limit of 160.
    Nothing was flagged near the fall.
  - play-profile pnw (fast, 2 years): 60 fps in every view, GPU 6–11 ms.
- **Records:** decision 0050 (waterfalls) and a line in AGENTS.md.
- **Limits:**
  - The ground is a grid of 8 m tiles with heights at the corners, so a fall can't have a sheer face.
    The ground under it is a steep ramp. From the front it reads as a fall over a ledge; from the side,
    the curtain arcs out over that ramp, and the gorge bank mostly hides it.
  - Old saves keep their land, so no fall. Only new PNW games get it.

## Open questions for Cole

1. **A sheerer fall?** Today it reads as a short, steep horsetail fall over a ledge, not a tall sheer
   drop.
   *Recommend: leave it. If you want more, draw a rock-ledge face at the lip (one small mesh per fall);
   that's drawing only, about a session's work.*
2. **Falls on other maps?** Real Atlanta has small falls (Cascade Springs), and Amazon terra firme
   streams have small cachoeiras. Neither map's creek has a steep reach today.
   *Recommend: no. Giving them falls would mean reshaping their land (0044), which changes balance for
   little gain.*
3. **Old PNW saves** have no fall.
   *Recommend: leave it. Heights are saved per farm, and reshaping a player's land on load would be
   surprising.*
4. **Fish can't go down the fall either** (real fish can). Nothing lives above it, since the steep
   reach above was already too shallow to swim.
   *Recommend: leave it.*
