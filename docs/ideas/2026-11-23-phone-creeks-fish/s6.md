# Session 6: Dug creeks drawn as a smooth ribbon

## Done

- **A one-tile creek's water now ends at a smooth ribbon along its course**, not in tile steps. This
  changes the drawing only. The simulation, the creek's tiles, its water levels, fish, plants and land
  don't move, and the flow shading from session 3 is untouched.
  - The game finds each creek's course again from its tiles, because courses aren't saved. Map creeks and
    dug ones are drawn the same way. It smooths the course and works out, at every tile corner, how far
    inside a ribbon 1.8 tiles wide that corner lies (`js/render3d/creek-ribbon.js`).
  - The water shader ends the water at the ribbon's edge, or at the ground's own waterline if that comes
    first. The foam and the fade at the edge follow it.
  - Heads narrow into a rounded end. Sumatra's canal junctions keep their right angles.
  - The ribbon stops two tiles before a pond, river, marsh, waterfall, wide stretch or beaver pond. There
    the water keeps its old outline, so a creek mouth flares into what it meets.
  - Record: decision 0055. It is also linked from 0054 and AGENTS.md.
- **No dry pits or flooded strips.** Wherever the creek's own bed is deeper than 0.2, the ribbon never
  hides its water, so no bare hole shows beside the creek. Bank ground that a creek on a slope used to
  flood is cut back to the ribbon. That tidies most of the downhill strip that looked flooded.
- **Measured.** I dug a tight S with brush 1 and measured the share of its outline that turns more than
  60° within a tile:

  | Map | Before | After |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | PNW | 23.4% | 3.7% |
  | Amazon | 6.1% | 3.2% |
  | Serengeti | 15.5% | 3.2% |
  | Atlanta | 7.3% | 1.6% |
  | Chinandega | 20.4% | 3.2% |
  | Sumatra | 8.1% | 2.7% |

  For comparison, the maps' own shores run 1-8%. Each map's creeks as generated came out the same or
  smoother. Dug heads now narrow to 0.59 of the creek's width, down from 0.92-0.97.
- **Checks.**
  - New `tools/creek-ribbon-check.mjs`, in the headless suite. It covers every creek map as generated
    plus a dug S, and checks that the outline is smooth, that no deep bed is left dry (heads included),
    and that heads narrow but keep their water. It also checks that working out the ribbon takes under
    40 ms. Measured: 2-5 ms per water rebuild.
  - These pass: the headless suite (148/148), `water-shape-check`, `water-check`, `waterfall-check`,
    `perf-guard-check`, `riverbank-check`, `canopy-support-check` and `fish-falls-marsh-check`.
  - `play-profile` (PNW and Serengeti, high quality, 2 years): 51-60 fps in the normal, wide and close
    views, and drawing 3-5 ms. Each frame only reads one more value per water corner.
  - A visual check on Serengeti and PNW, at the overview and the closest zoom, showed smooth outlines
    and no gaps, pits or floating edges. The S joins the creek cleanly at the north fence.
  - The regression reviewer went over the plan and the diff. It found that heads showed a dry pit, and
    that is fixed.
- **Kept self-contained for session 7.** It is one new module, about ten lines in `buildWater`, and one
  line in the water shader. The ribbon is worked out for the whole map on every water rebuild, so a
  patch-only redraw can take it as it is, or crop it later.

## Open questions for Cole

1. **Should a creek dug across a slope pile its spoil as a low bank on the downhill side?** I didn't
   build this. A real bank means raising the ground the simulation uses, because animals walk on it and
   water spreads over it. Those are terrain heights, not drawing. The ribbon already trims most of the
   flooded-looking strip. **Recommended:** no for now. Look at a dug hillside creek in play first. If the
   downhill bank still reads wrong, a short session can add spoil through the one land-edit path
   (`terrainEdit`, 0048), with beaver ponds' spreading checked.
2. **Should the ribbon fade out gradually where a creek meets a pond or river?** Right now it stops two
   tiles before them, so the creek widens over one tile at its mouth. That reads as a natural flare, but
   on a close look it can show as a short straight step. **Recommended:** leave it unless you notice it
   in play.
3. **On PNW at the overview, a dug creek reads as a grey strip in dim light.** It looks much like the
   map's own ditch, and the visual check couldn't compare it with the old look. **Recommended:** glance
   at it once in play. If it reads as a road rather than water, tune the creek's shallow colour on PNW,
   not the ribbon.

## New ideas noticed

- Fish, waders and drinking spots are placed anywhere in their tile, so near the outer corners of a
  diagonal reach they can sit just outside the drawn water, now that the water no longer runs out over
  the bank. A small follow-up could keep them inside the ribbon, using the same field.
- The ground's dark wet fringe beside the water still follows the ground's own heights. It reads as a
  soft, patchy halo rather than a staircase, but it could follow the ribbon as well.
- The ribbon is worked out for the whole map on every water rebuild. That is cheap now (2-5 ms), but
  session 7's patch redraw could recompute it only near the edit.
