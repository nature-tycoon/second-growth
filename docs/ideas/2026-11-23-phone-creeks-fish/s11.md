# Session 11: Water redraws only the edited patch

## Done

- **When you let go of a stroke beside water, only the water near the edit is redrawn.** That means the
  stretch of creek, its bank ring, shore heights and riverbank stones, or the one pond or marsh it
  touched. Before, every body of water on the map was rebuilt. The water builder moved to its own file,
  `js/render3d/water-build.js`. It keeps what it worked out last time and compares it with the map: the
  heights, and each tile's water (its terrain, and the beaver-pond model's flag and level). Then it redoes
  each step only as far as the change reaches:
  - the tiles' own levels;
  - any pond or marsh within 2 tiles, filled again whole (one body, one surface);
  - the bank ring and the corners;
  - the current's streak coordinates, but only for a stretch whose flow really changed;
  - the stones within 2 tiles, plus any whose plants or trees grew;
  - the creek ribbon, only if a creek tile's part in it changed.

  A whole rebuild still happens in four cases: the change covers more than 35% of the map, a beaver dam
  changed, the creek beyond the north fence went dry or wet, or the edit reaches the fence row on a map
  whose creek runs past the fence.
- **It matches a whole rebuild exactly, byte for byte.** A whole build and a patch run the same code; a
  whole build is just the patch with the window over the whole map. The new whole build also matches the
  old builder exactly on every map, both as generated and after 200 days of play.
- **Cost** (node, fast Mac, warm medians, a bank corner moved beside a creek / pond / river):

  | Map | Whole water build | Patch: creek | Patch: pond | Patch: river |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | PNW | 11.8 ms | 2.7 (23%) | 2.6 (22%) | 2.6 (22%) |
  | Amazon | 12.9 | 3.3 (25%) | 3.0 (23%) | 2.8 (22%) |
  | Serengeti | 12.1 | 2.8 (23%) | 2.9 (24%) | 2.8 (23%) |
  | Atlanta | 6.6 | 1.6 (24%) | – | 1.6 (25%) |
  | Chinandega | 14.8 | 3.9 (26%) | 4.6 (31%) | 3.8 (26%) |
  | Sumatra | 14.2 | 3.4 (24%) | – | 3.6 (25%) |

  The whole build itself got faster: 6.6–15 ms, down from 8–25 ms, with the same output. The streak
  coordinates now use typed arrays doing the same arithmetic, and water quality is worked out once per
  corner instead of once per vertex. So against the old whole rebuild, a patch costs 10–15%. What's left
  in a patch is whole on purpose because it's cheap: the channel flow field (1–1.5 ms) and the
  riverbanks' fingerprints and meshes (1–2.5 ms).
- **s7's open question 2 (the lag while dragging) is fixed without new code.** The renderer already
  holds the water for three times its last rebuild cost. At 2–5 ms that hold has ended by the next
  frame, so on a desktop the water follows a Raise/Lower drag nearly every frame, and the new bank no
  longer shows under the old water. A slow phone may still skip a frame or two (about 0.03 s).
- **The daily water refresh** (beaver ponds filling and drying) goes through the same patch, so it got
  cheaper too.
- **Checks.**
  - `terrain-patch-check` now has two more parts. The first is a fuzz: random changes near water, beyond
    what the tools allow, each drawn as a patch on one terrain and compared with a whole build on
    another. The changes are moved corners, including a creek's; land turned to pond, marsh or creek;
    water filled; beaver-pond levels nudged, including tiny ones; buildings and features on banks;
    trees and sedge grown; and ground repaints in between. It compares every water value, the shore
    heights, the drawn surface the animals swim by, the riverbank stones and the falls. A 250-step run
    gave about 1,100 patches, all exact. The suite runs 36 steps. The second part is the cost: under
    40% of a whole water build. Near-water strokes must now end as a patch.
  - Passed: the whole headless suite (153 of 153), `water-shape-check`, `creek-ribbon-check`,
    `offmap-creek-check`, `riverbank-check`, `waterfall-check`, `plant-shadow-check`,
    `ground-patch-check`, `water-check`, `terrain-edit-check`, `brush-render-check`, `flood-reach-check`
    and `terrain-relief-check`. `perf-guard-check` passed with no baseline change. In a real browser,
    `elevation-overlay-browser-check` and `tool-actions-browser-check` passed too.
  - `animal-trails-browser-check` fails ("visible ground tracks in Fast"), but it fails the same way on
    the commit before this session, so this work didn't cause it (see new ideas).
  - `play-profile pnw,serengeti high 2`: 58–60 fps before and after. Two PNW runs moved by up to 3 ms in
    opposite directions, in plant drawing and culling, not water, while Cole was using the Mac. No worse.
- **Reviewed.** The regression reviewer went over the plan and the diff.
  - The plan review caught three problems. Creek stretches start from the first creek-kind tile, which
    can be a dry bank tile, so the flow field is now redone whole each time (it's cheap). Writing in
    place would have hidden what changed from the shore comparison and the renderer. And cached stones
    would drift from live growth, so each bank tile now has its own fingerprint.
  - The diff review caught one: a tiny beaver-pond level change on a creek tile could leave the creek
    ribbon stale. It's fixed and now in the fuzz.
- Decision records 0048 (rules line and history) and 0055, AGENTS.md and the performance review are
  updated.

## Open questions for Cole

1. **Should a slow phone also stop holding the water during a drag?** On a phone the patch may take
   10–20 ms, so the hold can skip a frame or two of water while you drag beside it. The bank under the
   old water then shows for about 0.03 s. **Recommended:** no, keep the hold. Without it, every drag
   frame beside water costs that much more on a phone, which is worse than a 0.03 s lag. Look once
   during the next phone check.

## New ideas noticed

- `animal-trails-browser-check` fails on main's code as well ("visible ground tracks in Fast": the
  trails changed 0 pixels in both Fast and High). The browser suite on main would block the itch.io
  upload. It's worth a short session to find whether the trails or the check broke.
- The riverbanks' meshes are rebuilt whole on every water build (about 0.4 ms, plus the GPU upload). If
  the stones of untouched cells kept their instance slots, a patch could upload only the cells it
  changed.
- The channel flow field could be redone only for the creek stretches the edit touches (about 1–1.5 ms
  saved). That needs the start-tile rule above copied exactly, so it was left whole.
- A change wider than 35% of the map, or one at a PNW-style fence row, still builds whole. The fence
  row could be patched too, if the north creek's ribbon block were cached.
