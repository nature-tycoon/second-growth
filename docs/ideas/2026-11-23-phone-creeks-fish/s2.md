# Session 2: Creek brush lag and S-curves

## Done

- **Measured the lag first.** The simulation side is cheap (a whole 12-tile stroke: under 1 ms of
  digging, 10-15 ms at the end). The lag had two causes:
  1. The creek's steering head runs 5 tiles behind the cursor so it can cut corners round (0054). The
     first 4 samples after the start dug nothing, so nothing showed.
  2. Every dug tile made the renderer rebuild the whole map's ground and water. On a fast Mac that
     made every frame of the drag 50-90 ms.
- **The stroke shows under the brush at once.** While you drag the creek tool, the tiles dug so far
  and the path from the head on to the brush are tinted water-blue (`pendingCourse` in
  `js/maps/water-shape.js`, used in `js/input.js`). The creek still bends no tighter than before. The
  dug channel fills in behind the blue path a few tiles back, and the shore shaping runs once when you
  let go.
- **The drag stays smooth.** During a creek stroke, the whole-map rebuild waits until three times its
  last cost has passed (`creekStroke` in `js/render3d/scene.js`). In a 14-tile drag that cut it from
  11 rebuilds to 4. After you let go, it redraws at once.
- **Checked.** A new `water-shape-check` case confirms the brush tile is dug or shown after every
  sample, on every map that has room for it. A real mouse drag in headless Chrome showed the blue path
  under the brush mid-stroke, with no errors. Records: 0054 (rules), performance review (open fix).
- **S-curves: measured, not fixed.** A tight brush-1 S still draws tile-stepped. The share of its
  waterline turning more than 60° within a tile: PNW 12.9%, Amazon 10.2%, Serengeti 13.1% (the maps'
  own shores: 1-8%). The cause is not the course, which is a smooth curve. It is the creek's banks:
  0054 keeps a creek's banks only 0.03 above the water so beaver ponds and floods can spread. So the
  drawn waterline runs out almost to the next tile's edge, and that edge is a tile staircase.

## Open questions for Cole

1. **How should a dug creek's S-curve be made smooth?** Two ways were measured:
   - Raising a dug creek's banks like a pond's (at most 0.7 m) took Serengeti from 13% to 3.5% and
     Amazon from 10% to 6.5%. It did nothing on PNW (steep slopes), and the reviewer showed it would
     stop beaver ponds from spreading on the creeks players dig to bring beavers in. Not built.
   - The real fix is a drawing change: draw the creek's water as a smooth ribbon along its course, not
     one flat sheet per tile. 0054 set that idea aside because the land, fish and shore plants stay on
     tiles.

   **Recommended:** a session that draws dug creeks' water as a smooth ribbon (drawing only; fish and
   plants stay on tiles). That is the only way that also fixes PNW.
2. **Should Raise/Lower get the same smooth drag?** They rebuild the whole map every frame too, but
   holding their redraw back slides the brush off the cursor. **Recommended:** yes, through the real
   fix: redraw only the edited patch of ground and water (logged as an open fix in the performance
   review). It is a medium-sized renderer session.
3. **Is the blue path the right look?** It shows where the stroke went, and the creek follows a few
   tiles behind, rounding corners. **Recommended:** keep it, and look at it once in play. If the gap
   between the blue path and the dug channel reads as lag, shorten the head's look-ahead from 5 tiles
   to 3 (corners would come out a little tighter).

## New ideas noticed

- A creek dug across a slope floods a tile-wide strip of its downhill bank in the drawing (each tile's
  water is a flat sheet). Real ditches dug across a hillside pile their spoil on the downhill side, so
  a low spoil bank there would be realistic and would also tidy the drawing.
- The end-of-stroke frame is still one long whole-map rebuild (about 100 ms on a fast Mac). A
  patch-only redraw would remove it for every land tool.
