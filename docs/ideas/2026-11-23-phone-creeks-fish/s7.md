# Session 7: Land tools redraw only the edited patch

## Done

- **Every land stroke now redraws only the land that moved**, not the whole map. The drawing keeps a
  copy of the heights it last drew and compares it with the map's after each change, so it finds
  exactly which corners moved, whatever moved them (any land tool, Undo, a flood cutting off a bend).
  It then rewrites the ground's tiles round those corners, the colour overlay, and the farm's edge
  line or the map's outer skirt only if the patch reaches them (`Terrain.reshape`,
  `js/render3d/terrain.js`). The ground repaint (colours, snow, wet shore fringe) now takes that patch
  too. Before, any height change refused a patch.
- **The brush stays on the cursor.** The ground is redrawn on the very frame of each sample, for every
  tool. Only the water may wait a moment (below).
- **The water is left alone when the edit is away from it.** It reads the ground's heights only within
  a few tiles of itself. So an edit more than 4 tiles from any water, with no water dug or filled,
  leaves the water mesh exactly as it was and skips its rebuild. Near water, or when water is dug, it
  is rebuilt whole, as before. While you drag any brush, that rebuild waits up to three times its last
  cost, as the creek tool's whole-map rebuild did since session 2. When you let go it catches up at
  once.
- **No seams.** Every value written is the one a whole-map redraw writes. The new check proves it byte
  for byte.
- **Measured** (node, fast Mac; `tools/terrain-patch-check.mjs --report`):

  The reef has no land tools.
- **Checks.** New `tools/terrain-patch-check.mjs`, in the headless suite. On every map it drags Raise,
  Lower and Fill & grade on dry land, Raise and Lower beside water, and a creek and a pond dug far from
  water, through the real tool actions, and follows each sample as the renderer does. Then it checks:
  - everything drawn equals a fresh whole-map redraw: ground, overlay, skirt, farm line, height range
    and every water value;
  - every changed ground position and normal is inside the range sent to the graphics chip;
  - the drag frames and the dry ends are under a quarter of a whole rebuild; a near-water end is one
    water rebuild and no whole-map redraw. That end is checked by what it does, not by time: timed
    once, cold, it failed at random under load.
- **Other checks.** The headless suite passes, as do `terrain-edit-check`, `water-shape-check`,
  `perf-guard-check` (no baseline change), `ground-patch-check`, `brush-render-check` (its stub terrain
  got a `reshape`), `riverbank-check`, `creek-ribbon-check` and `water-check`.
  `play-profile` (PNW and Serengeti, high, 2 years), before → after:
  - PNW play: 54 → 59 fps; Serengeti play: 60 → 60 fps.
  - Drawing: 3-7 ms before, 3-5 ms after. The game doesn't edit land in that profile, so this is run noise.
  - No worse.

  Decision records 0048 and 0054 and the performance review are updated.
- **Reviewed.** The regression reviewer went over the plan and the diff. Its plan review caught that a
  creek dug far from water would never be drawn; a second water key without the heights version covers
  that. Its diff review caught that a drag starting beside a pond and moving onto dry land could drop the
  held water rebuild. That is fixed (`waterPending`) and now has a "near to dry" stroke in the check. It
  also had smaller fixes: upload ranges are added, not replaced; a new day hands its shore change to the
  ground repaint.
- **In a real browser.** `elevation-overlay-browser-check` passes: the overlay is never missing over 41
  reshaped frames of Raise/Lower drags and Undo, and the recolour takes a median of 1.7 ms.
  `tool-actions-browser-check` passes with real mouse and phone strokes.

## Smaller than asked, and why

The brief asked for the water to be patched too. I measured it first. Most of a water rebuild works
over the whole map by design:
- the riverbank stones and branches are re-scattered (~24%);
- the current's coordinates are relaxed along each whole stretch of creek so the streaks don't seam
  (~16%, session 3's flow shading);
- ponds that touch are given one surface;
- the creek ribbon (~7%).

Making all of that incremental is a rewrite of the water builder, about the size of this session again,
with risk to sessions 3 and 6's water look. So the near-water end of a stroke still costs one water
rebuild: roughly half of the old whole-map rebuild, not a small fraction.

## Open questions for Cole

1. **Should the water redraw only its patch too?** Today, digging or shaping beside water still costs
   one whole water rebuild when you let go (15-85 ms; a short hitch on a phone).
   **Recommended:** yes, as its own session. Re-scatter riverbank stones only near the edit, and rebuild
   only the stretch of creek or the pond the edit touched, checked byte for byte against a whole rebuild
   as this session's check does.
2. **Is the brief wait of the water while dragging beside a pond acceptable?** For up to about 0.1 s
   during a Raise/Lower drag next to water, the new bank can show under the old water sheet. It is exact
   the moment you let go. The same wait now applies while painting a pond or marsh, where before the
   water was rebuilt on every sample; the dug pit shows at once. **Recommended:** yes. Look at it once in play. If it reads as a flicker, rebuild
   the water every frame for Raise/Lower near small ponds only.

## New ideas noticed

- Every stroke's end still repaints the whole map's ground colours once, because the tool's end asks for
  it ("shade and moisture reach beyond the stroke", `ui.onToolAction`). That is about 6-12 ms in node. It
  could repaint only the area the environment refresh changed.
- The flood mesh reads the ground's heights but is only rebuilt once a day, so floodwater over land you
  just reshaped keeps its old shape until the next day. This is not new; it was so before.
- Demolish never changes heights (roads and gravel go to bare soil at the same height), so it never
  needed a height redraw.
