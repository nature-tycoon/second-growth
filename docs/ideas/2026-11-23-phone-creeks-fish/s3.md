# Session 3: Waterfall water flow direction

## Done

- **Measured first.** The drawn current on every creek corner within 8 tiles of PNW's fall points
  downhill. That holds on the fresh map and with creeks dug beside the fall, joining below the pool and
  above the lip, plus a pond by the pool. So the map data was fine and the bug was in the water shader.
- **Cause: two layers of the surface shading ran the wrong way.**
  1. The ripple layers that tilt the light on the water read their noise at "position + current x time".
     That moves the pattern *against* the current, so on every creek they drifted upstream. The streaks
     and the white water read it the other way, which is why the waterfall itself looked right.
  2. The small surface waves drifted one fixed way (north-west) over the whole map. PNW's creek runs
     south, so there they ran upstream too.
- **Fix (drawing only, `js/render3d/terrain-materials.js`).** The ripples now drift with the current. On
  moving water the waves are laid out along the creek and travel downstream. Ponds keep their one-way,
  wind-like drift. Two of the three waves were turned a little so all three travel within 25° of each
  other. Before, one ran 71° off and crossed the creek sideways where a side creek joins.
- **Checked.** `waterfall-check` has a new case. It measures the current and every wave against the bed's
  real downhill at every corner round the fall, on the fresh map and on the edited one. It also asserts
  the ripple direction in the shader. It fails on the old shader. In headless Chrome the shader compiles
  clean, and the pattern moves downstream above and below the fall (about 0.08 tiles/s, frames
  cross-correlated). water, hollis-water, dug-creek, bare-soil, riverbank and perf-guard checks all pass,
  and geometry is unchanged. Rule added to decision 0050.

## Open questions for Cole

1. **Should ponds' ripples drift at all?** They still slide slowly one way, like a light wind, on every
   pond and lake. **Recommended:** keep it. Still water with a breeze looks alive, and nothing reads it
   as "flowing".
2. **Faster-looking water in steep reaches?** The drift speed is the same on a gentle creek and the
   steep reach above the fall (only river vs creek differ). **Recommended:** leave it for now. Speed by
   slope is a small follow-up if the steep reach still looks lazy to you.

## New ideas noticed

- Where a side creek joins, the drawn current turns toward the main creek about two tiles up the side
  creek (up to 57° off its own downhill). It reads as water starting to turn, which is fine. A tighter
  turn would need less smoothing at mouths.
- The visual-qa strip measurement (pixels along the creek, cross-correlated between two shader times)
  could become a browser check for flow direction on any map.
