# Session 10: Wire tree guards for planted saplings

## Done

- **Tree guard tool** (Habitat → Tree guard, $5 a tree at Standard, brushed over trees). It's a wire cage like the ones restoration crews put round riparian plantings. Beavers can't fell a guarded tree, and deer and elk can't browse it, tread it down or fray it in the rut. Elephants still can, because a cage doesn't stop an elephant. It goes on any young tree on land, planted or wild, and not on the reef (its "trees" are corals). Everything lives in `js/sim/tree-guard.js`. The rules are in a new decision record, [0057](../../decisions/0057-wire-tree-guards.md).
- **The guard comes off by the tree's age (8 years), not its size. This is a change from the brief.** The brief asked for a guard that comes off once the tree is "big enough". I measured first: on the opening's planting, most trees beavers took were *full grown* (size 1.0, years 4 to 10), not saplings. Planted trees reach full size in about 3 years, so a size limit would have protected almost nothing. A real cage also comes off when the trunk fills it, which is age. On a grown tree, the cage reads as a wire ring round the trunk base, which is how real trunk guards look.
- **The offer.** It comes once per farm, on the first tree you planted that beavers cut. It shows as a notice with a "Tree guard" button that puts the tool in your hand. The wording says beavers eating the wild trees is fine, and only yours need it. In the campaign, the tool unlocks with the offer.
- **The time-lapse stops for it.** The opening's ten-year time-lapse is where beavers first reach the planting, in years 3 to 8. An offer shown only after it would come once the trees were already gone. So the time-lapse stops there once, the way it already stops for a fire in the planting. It flies to the cut tree and shows the offer. See question 1.
- **The offer is never lost.** If the time-lapse ends some other way in the same frame (the last day, the newcomer's few seconds, money, fire), the offer is shown as it ends. In the campaign, the toolbar redraws so the Habitat group isn't left dimmed. The regression reviewer found these two problems in the diff, plus a deer-browse path on the hard difficulty and a guard that could outlive its tree on load; all four are fixed.
- **Save/load and undo carry the guards.** Older saves load with none. A guard never outlives its tree: removing the tree, clearing the tile or planting a new one clears the guard, and a load drops any guard left on a tile with no tree.
- **Drawing:** a small cage of thin wire bars at the trunk (`treeGuard` in `render3d/geometry/features.js`, one `treeguard` pool in flora.js, a plain band at the far zoom). A guard going on or coming off redraws only its tile.
- **Checks:**
  - New `tools/tree-guard-check.mjs` (in the headless suite). Beavers chewing round a mixed stand cut 83 of 83 unguarded trees and 0 of 84 guarded ones. In a hard-grazed sward, 16 of 16 guarded seedlings live through a year and 0 of 16 unguarded ones do. Deer on the hard difficulty leave guarded saplings alone. The guard comes off at 8 years. The check also covers the price, undo, save/load (old saves included), a tree that's gone on load, the offer firing once, and no tool on the reef.
  - `opening-lapse-check` now plays a player who takes the offer the day it comes; `--no-guards` gives the old behaviour.
  - Over 8 run seeds (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 11, 13), every seed passes on its own: 29 to 51 trees on the planted stretch after ten years, median 38. Session 5b had the low seeds at 14 to 19.
  - Guards cost the player $45 to $250 on those runs.
- **Balance:** seeded `calibrate all 15 3`, before and after: nothing moved on any of the seven maps (no guards are placed in those runs, and unguarded tiles draw the same random numbers as before).
- **Suite:** the full headless suite passed, 153 of 153. `perf-guard-check` passes; its fixture has no guards, so it can't count the cage's cost. Measured in a preview instead: the cage pool doesn't exist until a guard does, and six guards cost one draw call and about 1,500 triangles. The cages sit on the ground, centred on the trunk, and are a small mark at the overview. No console errors.

## Open questions for Cole

1. **Should the first-visit time-lapse stop when beavers cut one of your new trees?** On PNW that happens in most first visits, somewhere in years 3 to 8. So most players would see the time-lapse stop early, with the offer, instead of watching all ten years. They'd still get the closing summary line, but not the "newcomer" animal shown at the end. *Recommend:* keep the stop. It's the only way the guards can save the opening's planting, and the fire stop already works the same way. The other choice is to let the ten years finish and offer guards afterwards. That's a one-line change, but the trees are mostly gone by then.
2. **Is 8 years right for the guard to come off?** Real riparian guards stay on 5 to 10 years, until the trunk fills them. At 8, a planting guarded early stays protected through most of the time-lapse. On a seed where beavers arrive late (year 8), most of the planting is already too old to take a guard. *Recommend:* keep 8.
3. **Should a guard on a grown tree stay longer?** Crews wrap mature cottonwoods beside beaver ponds for good. *Recommend:* no. Cole asked that mature forest not be protected permanently, and the unguarded grown trees are the beavers' real food.

## New ideas noticed

- A guarded tree still counts as beaver food (`beaverFood` in `sim/beaver-ponds.js`). So a colony can settle beside a planting it can't eat, and cut more wild trees round it. That's realistic, so I left it. A colony that has eaten everything it can reach should move on sooner.
- A guarded tree still counts as browse forage for deer in `w.browse`. That makes a big guarded planting look like more food than it is. It's small, but the forage map could leave guarded trees out.
- `fellAtDam` still clears guarded trees where a dam's ends meet the bank. Beavers really do take caged trees when building, but only rarely. Fine as is.
- `first-minutes-browser-check` (browser suite) expects the first time-lapse to end 'done'. It fakes the ten years by moving the start day, so a beaver cut is unlikely within its few real days. I didn't run it. If it ever ends 'guards', that's this stop.
