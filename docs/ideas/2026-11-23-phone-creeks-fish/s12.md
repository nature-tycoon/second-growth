# Session 12: stranded air-breathing fish wriggle to the next water

## Done

- **Air-breathing fish wriggle out of a puddle.** New trait `breathesAir`, given to the giant snakehead (Sumatra) and the African catfish (Serengeti), whose field-guide text already says it wriggles between pools.
  - A fish with the trait, in water that holds 2 tiles deep enough for it or fewer, crosses to the nearest bigger water within 3 tiles. It goes in a straight line over wet ground, with at most 2.2 tiles of it out of the water.
  - Wet ground is water, marsh or mud, or soil soaked to moisture 0.75 or more (Sumatra's peat floor is 0.97). It never crosses road, gravel, a trail, a building or dry land.
  - In the game it sets off at night by the drawn sun, or in the rain.
  - On the ground it moves at about a third of its swimming speed, and it doesn't jump back into the pool behind it.
  - Code: `js/sim/fish-wriggle.js`, called from `chooseTarget`, `riverFishStep`/`walkStep` and steering.
- **Fish without the trait die off as their pool dries.** A fish in water shallower than it needs, with no deep water it can reach that day, dies at a quarter chance a day (`strandedDies`). An air-breather dies only once its spot holds no water at all.
  - Before this, a stranded fish never died. Measured over a year on six maps, no fish was ever left with no water at all.
- **Drawing.** While it crosses, the fish lies belly-down on the ground (it was drawn half sunk into it) and its body wave beats three times as fast, so it reads as a wriggle. Every other fish draws exactly as before.

   - *Recommend:* leave it. If the motion sweep ever flags a fish jump, let a fish stuck on the ground that long die instead.

## New ideas noticed

- Real snakeheads and Clarias also bury into the mud and wait out a drought when their pool dries completely. An air-breather left with no water and nowhere to go could lie dormant in the mud until the rains (the dormancy state already exists), rather than staying put or dying.
- Chinandega's snappers are often in small cut-off pools (75–166 of about 2–3 thousand samples). They don't breathe air, so they wait there for the water to rise. It's worth a look whether those pools ever dry out under them in a bad dry season, now that stranded fish can die.
- The pile probe counts a few fish swimming through each other in open water as a pile (seed 1987 after). Counting only fish standing still would separate real piles from routes crossing.
